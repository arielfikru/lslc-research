In-Place Editable Context Memory vs. Raw-Text RAG: The Math Behind 27 MiB at 32K

Category:

Guides

Published:

Nov 23, 2026

Written by:

Ariel Fikru

This is the second wall every self-hosted LLM deployment hits right after solving weight memory: you quantize a 4B or 8B model so it loads comfortably on one GPU, you open the context window to 32K or 128K tokens for multi-turn coding agents, and within twenty turns both Full KV-cache and Raw-Text Chunk RAG run into trouble. Full KV eats gigabytes; standard Raw-Text Chunk RAG trims context by retrieving top-\(k\) chunks, but the moment a user says "wait—change the budget cap from 10M to 60M and move the staging endpoint to Frankfurt", you cannot revise chunk 3 in place. Immutable chunks serve stale constraints, appended errata confuse the model, and LLM chunk rewrites pay full decode latency per edit.

So this guide derives what it takes to replace an append-only history with an editable, multi-resolution living book—Layered Spatial Latent Caching (LSLC)—compared directly against Raw-Text Chunk RAG (with Full BF16 KV as the uncompressed context ceiling). There are only three buckets in a compressed book's memory budget at inference time, and two of them cost pennies. The third—the active KV cache of the projected tokens handed to the LLM—decides both when LSLC beats Constant-\(k\) RAG and why a 2-level hierarchical TOC becomes mandatory above ~100K tokens.

TL;DR

  • Even with Grouped-Query Attention (GQA), a standard BF16 KV cache costs 112 KiB per token on Qwen3-1.7B and 144 KiB per token on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B (the uncompressed ceiling: 3.50 to 4.50 GiB at 32K; 28 to 36 GiB at 256K).
  • LSLC compiles each 256-token page into a [L=3, C=128, H=4, W=4] 2D latent sheet (12 KiB/page) paired with a 16-slot Sparse Exact-Recall Footnote Bank (128 B/page).
  • Head-to-head vs. Raw-Text Chunk RAG at 32K (Qwen3-1.7B, L4): LSLC uses 27.11 MiB total VRAM (218 active tokens; 54.6 ms Warm TTFT) vs. 56.1 MiB for Constant-\(k=2\) RAG (zero TOC tokens, 512 raw tokens analytical) and 71.72–183.72 MiB for shared-TOC Deep-2 / Deep-2+Skim-4 Raw RAG (634–1,658 active tokens; 62.6–159.4 ms TTFT)—or 132.3× below the Full BF16 KV ceiling.
  • The ~100K Constant-\(k\) RAG Crossover & 2-Level TOC: Above 384–392 pages (~98.4K–100.4K tokens), a flat 1-level TOC overtakes Constant-\(k=2\) RAG (73.66 MiB vs. 56.6 MiB at 128K). Our implemented 2-Level Chapter\(\rightarrow\)Page Hierarchy (chapter_size=16, \(\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0\)) keeps analytical net VRAM under Constant-\(k=2\) RAG through 256K (\(\le 23.3\) MiB at 128K; \(\le 32.9\) MiB at 256K).
  • In-Place Editability: Volatility-ordered prefix caching lets DeltaUpdater overwrite \(\ell=2\) in place across \(k=3\) numeric overrides at 100.0% ± 0.0% EM (3 seeds) and \(k=5\) symbolic overrides at 80.0% (cold)–91.7% (k=3-warmed) EM—vs. 0.0% Immutable RAG and 38.3%–52.1% Append-Errata RAG (Raw-Text In-Place LLM-Rewrite RAG + shared non-oracle Okapi BM25 are queued in scripts/colab_run_rebuttal_v2.py).

The context memory budget

When you replace a flat 1D KV cache with a routed latent book, everything resident on the GPU on top of the frozen model weights falls into three buckets:

$$M_{\text{context}} = \underbrace{P \cdot (L \cdot C \cdot H \cdot W \cdot b_{\text{lat}})}_{\text{3D latent book}} + \underbrace{P \cdot (K_{\text{exact}} \cdot 8\text{ B})}_{\text{exact footnote bank}} + \underbrace{N_{\text{active}}(P) \cdot M_{\text{KV/tok}}}_{\text{active LLM KV cache}}$$

Here \(P = \lceil N / 256 \rceil\) is the number of 256-token pages, \([L, C, H, W] = [3, 128, 4, 4]\) is the 3D spatial-layer tensor per page stored at \(b_{\text{lat}} = 2\) bytes (FP16/BF16), \(K_{\text{exact}} = 16\) is the number of high-salience verbatim tokens kept per page as 8-byte discrete records, and \(N_{\text{active}}(P)\) is the number of projected soft-prompt and footnote tokens actually entering the frozen LLM's attention layers on the current query.

Many context-compression papers report only the first term (12 KiB per page) and claim \(2{,}000\times\) compression. That is an illusion. The moment you project compressed memory slots back into a frozen Transformer so it can attend to them, those projected tokens populate a standard active KV cache of size \(N_{\text{active}}(P) \cdot M_{\text{KV/tok}}\). In real serving, that third term is 90% of your context footprint, so every calculation below counts all three.

The GQA baseline: why flat KV still bites

For any Transformer with \(N_{\text{lay}}\) layers, \(n_{\text{kv}}\) key/value heads, head dimension \(d_{\text{head}}\), and \(b_{\text{kv}} = 2\) bytes per element (FP16/BF16), each active cached token costs:

$$M_{\text{KV/tok}} = 2 \cdot N_{\text{lay}} \cdot n_{\text{kv}} \cdot d_{\text{head}} \cdot b_{\text{kv}}$$

All three Qwen3 backbones in our benchmark share \(n_{\text{kv}} = 8\) KV heads and \(d_{\text{head}} = 128\), so their per-token KV cost depends only on layer depth:

Backbone Layers / Hidden BF16 KV / token 32K flat KV 256K flat KV
Qwen3-1.7B 28 / 2,048 112 KiB 3,584.0 MiB (3.50 GiB) 28,672.0 MiB (28.0 GiB)
Qwen3-4B 36 / 2,560 144 KiB 4,608.0 MiB (4.50 GiB) 36,864.0 MiB (36.0 GiB)
Qwen3-8B (4-bit NF4 W) 36 / 4,096 144 KiB 4,608.0 MiB (4.50 GiB) 36,864.0 MiB (36.0 GiB)

Notice two things. First, quantizing Qwen3-8B weights to 4-bit NF4 does not shrink its runtime KV cache: past_key_values remain in BF16 at 144 KiB per token, identical to Qwen3-4B. Second, because modern GQA already compresses KV cache down to 112–144 KiB per token (28–36 KiB in ideal INT4), any soft-prompt compressor that emits 25% as many tokens as the original prompt fails to beat plain INT4 KV quantization. To win by two orders of magnitude, you must shrink \(N_{\text{active}}(P)\) without blinding the model to unretrieved pages.

Turning the stone scroll into a multi-resolution book

A flat 1D KV cache behaves like a 10-kilometer stone scroll: linear to scan and impossible to revise in the middle. Human knowledge systems solved long-horizon memory by inventing the book: modular pages (\(P\)), a persistent 1-line Table of Contents (TOC) per page, coarse skimming, deep reading on the two pages that matter right now, margin sticky-notes for updates, and a back-of-book footnote index for exact numbers and identifiers.

LSLC compiles that structure into GPU memory. For a 128-page (32,768-token) book, a lightweight QueryToTOCRouter scores the query against all 128 TOC tokens and routes top-\(k_{\text{deep}}=2\) pages to Deep Read, next-\(k_{\text{skim}}=4\) pages to Skim, and the remaining 122 pages to \(1\times 1\) TOC:

$$N_{\text{active}}(128) = \underbrace{122 \times 1}_{\text{TOC } (1\times 1)} + \underbrace{4 \times 4}_{\text{Skim } (2\times 2)} + \underbrace{2 \times (4 + 4 + 16)}_{\text{Deep } (\ell=0,1,2)} + \underbrace{2 \times 16}_{\text{Exact footnotes}} = 218\text{ tokens}$$

Plug \(P = 128\) and \(N_{\text{active}} = 218\) into our three-bucket formula on Qwen3-4B (144 KiB/token):

$$M_{\text{LSLC}}(32\text{K}) = \underbrace{1.50\text{ MiB}}_{\text{128 pages } \times 12\text{ KiB}} + \underbrace{0.016\text{ MiB}}_{\text{128 pages } \times 128\text{ B}} + \underbrace{30.66\text{ MiB}}_{218 \times 144\text{ KiB active KV}} = 32.17\text{ MiB analytical} \; (34.42\text{ MiB live CUDA})$$

Why editability—not compression alone—is the real prize

If your only goal were throwing away 99% of the KV cache on static prose, StreamingLLM already does that with zero training by keeping a 128-token window—at the cost of going completely blind (0.0% Exact NIAH) to anything evicted earlier. And if your only goal were retrieving two static pages verbatim, Constant-\(k=2\) Raw-Text RAG keeps active KV fixed at 512 raw tokens (~56.1–57.1 MiB on 1.7B) with zero TOC tokens, while shared-TOC Deep-2+Skim-4 Raw RAG reaches 1,658 active tokens (183.72 MiB).

Where both read-only latent compressors and Raw-Text Chunk RAG struggle is in-place state revision. When a user revises an earlier constraint three to five times across a conversation (\(t_1 \to t_2 \to t_3\)), immutable Raw-Text RAG reads the outdated initial page (0.0% EM), while append-errata RAG concatenates every edit onto the text chunk—leaving stale and current values co-existing in the prompt (52.1% EM on \(k=3\), 38.3% on \(k=5\) across 3 seeds). Scope note: v1 baselines cover Immutable and Append-Errata RAG only; live In-Place LLM-Rewrite RAG accuracy and per-edit latency (ms/edit) alongside shared non-oracle Okapi BM25 retrieval (N=180) are queued in scripts/colab_run_rebuttal_v2.py.

LSLC solves this by separating each page into Static (\(\ell=0\)), Entity (\(\ell=1\)), and Dynamic sticky-note (\(\ell=2\)) layers, ordering the projected soft-prompt prefix monotonically by volatility, and overwriting \(\ell=2\) in place via convex gated interpolation (\(\mathbf{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{new}} = (1 - \mathbf{G}_p) \odot \mathbf{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{old}} + \mathbf{G}_p \odot \Delta\mathbf{Z}_{p, 2}\)): hitting 100.0% ± 0.0% EM on 3-seed \(k=3\) numeric overrides and 80.0% (cold)–91.7% (k=3-warmed) EM on \(k=5\) symbolic overrides while keeping \(\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0\).

The Constant-k RAG crossover and 2-level hierarchy

Look at our measured live CUDA VRAM across context lengths on Qwen3-1.7B: 12.88 MiB at 4K, 27.11 MiB at 32K, 73.66 MiB at 128K, and 135.72 MiB at 256K. Notice a critical comparison against Constant-\(k=2\) Raw-Text RAG (0 TOC tokens in LLM KV; strictly 512 raw tokens retrieved = 56.14–57.12 MiB on 1.7B across 32K–256K): a flat 1-level TOC emits one active KV token per page, causing flat LSLC to cross above Constant-\(k=2\) RAG at \(P^* = 384.3\) pages (~98.4K tokens on 1.7B) and \(P^* = 392.1\) pages (~100.4K tokens on 4B/8B). Above 100K tokens, a 2-level hierarchy is therefore mandatory, not optional.

Grouping every 16 pages into a Chapter via our 2-Level Chapter\(\rightarrow\)Page Hierarchy (hierarchical_chapter_size=16 in src/lslc/projector.py, verified \(\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0\)) caps analytical active prefix tokens at \(\le 134\) (32K), \(\le 158\) (128K), and \(\le 190\) (256K)—holding Qwen3-1.7B analytical net VRAM to \(\le 16.2\text{ MiB}\) (32K), \(\le 23.3\text{ MiB}\) (128K), and \(\le 32.9\text{ MiB}\) (256K), strictly smaller than Constant-\(k=2\) RAG throughout 256K. Against Full BF16 KV alone, a 1-level TOC also hits an analytical limit of \(\frac{28{,}672\text{ KiB}}{124.125\text{ KiB}} = 231.0\times\) (1.7B) / \(236.2\times\) (4B/8B) before hierarchical grouping unlocks 1M+ contexts.

A recipe you can follow

  • Count active soft-prompt KV bytes first: multiply projected tokens \(N_{\text{active}}\) by \(2 \cdot L \cdot n_{\text{kv}} \cdot d_{\text{head}} \cdot 2\). If \(N_{\text{active}} > 0.20 \cdot N\), plain INT4 KV quantization will beat you on memory with zero training.
  • Order your prefix by volatility: place immutable global TOC tokens first, structural facts (\(\ell \in \{0,1\}\)) in the middle, and mutable working state (\(\ell=2\) + footnotes) last so prefix caches survive edits by construction.
  • Add an 8-byte/token discrete footnote bypass (token_id: int32, page_id: uint16, h: uint8, w: uint8) for the top 6.25% high-salience tokens and gather embeddings directly from the frozen LLM's embed_tokens table on Deep Read.
  • Attenuate runner-up page footnotes by relative router confidence \((p_{\text{deep}}/p_{\max})^2\) so background numbers from page #2 do not corrupt single-page needle copy.
  • Reconsolidate a page after 4 consecutive in-place overwrites: re-encoding one 256-token page takes ~1.8 ms (\(O(1)\) in total context length) and prevents the 1–2 character cumulative drift seen at \(k=5\) overwrites on 4-bit backbones.

Frequently asked questions

Isn't LSLC just RAG with extra steps?

No. Standard Constant-\(k=2\) Raw-Text Chunk RAG is blind to unretrieved pages, re-prefills 512 raw tokens per query (~56.1–57.1 MiB on Qwen3-1.7B; up to 183.72 MiB when skim pages are included), and struggles with in-place state revisions (0.0% immutable, 38.3%–52.1% with appended errata). LSLC keeps all \(P\) pages visible via a differentiable Matryoshka pyramid (switching automatically to a 2-level Chapter\(\rightarrow\)Page hierarchy above ~100K tokens so it stays under Constant-\(k\) RAG through 256K) and overwrites \(\ell=2\) in place at 100.0% \(k=3\) EM (80.0% cold / 91.7% warmed \(k=5\) EM). Note that v1 baselines do not yet time LLM-rewrite RAG per edit or decouple Okapi BM25 retrieval; both are implemented in scripts/colab_run_rebuttal_v2.py.

Why does a 12 KiB continuous page lose exact IDs when 256 token IDs only cost 1 KiB?

Because 256 int32 token IDs (1 KiB) are discrete lookup pointers into a vocabulary table, whereas a 12 KiB continuous tensor must project into distributed \(d_{\text{llm}}\)-dimensional hidden states. Expecting a continuous soft-prompt projector trained in 300 steps to act as a bit-exact hash table for random 9-token codes is an optimization alignment bottleneck, which a 128-byte/page discrete footnote bank solves cleanly.

Why did Episode Exact Match (90.0%) exceed BPE sub-token capture (81.8%) on Python code?

Because 81.82% (54/66) measures the fraction of individual BPE sub-tokens stored in the 16-slot discrete bank, whereas 90.0% (18/20) is full-episode answer accuracy. On multi-token identifiers like half_open_probe, capturing the leading sub-tokens in the discrete bank anchors the frozen LLM while the 24 continuous 3D latent tokens of that page complete the trailing sub-token.

Does carrying a compressed LSLC book prefix hurt multi-step reasoning?

On Qwen3-8B, context-grounded multi-step reasoning across the compressed book (2-step arithmetic, Python if/else branch tracing, and 3-entity transitive deduction) reaches 100.0% accuracy, matching Full BF16 KV (100.0% retention).

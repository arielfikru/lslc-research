\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) over raw text chunks avoids quadratic Full-KV prefill compute ($O(N^2)$) and linear KV-cache memory growth ($O(N \cdot d_{\text{kv}})$) by retrieving top-$k$ pages per query, yet remains difficult to update in place across multi-turn revisions: immutable chunks serve stale constraints, appending errata leaves conflicting history in the prompt, deterministic regex text-patching requires rigid schemas and fails ($0.0\%$) on paraphrased natural-language revisions, and LLM-based chunk rewrites incur $487$--$14{,}631\,\text{ms/edit}$ autoregressive decode latency. Conversely, recent 1D latent compressors (\textbf{LCLMs}, \textbf{Memorilla}) are read-only and lose exact verbatim identifiers, while editable KV methods (\emph{Models Take Notes at Prefill}) operate over uncompressed standard KV caches. We introduce \textbf{Layered Spatial Latent Caching (LSLC)}, compiling each $256$-token page into a multi-layer 2D latent tensor $\vect{Z}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 128 \times 4 \times 4}$ ($12\,\text{KiB/page}$) paired with a $128\,\text{byte/page}$ \textbf{Sparse Exact-Recall Footnote Bank}. LSLC combines: (1)~\textbf{Volatility-Ordered Prefix Caching} across an immutable structural bank ($\text{TOC}$, \emph{Skim}, and static/entity layers $\ell \in \{0,1\}$) and a mutable tail bank ($\ell=2$), using a 2-level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page TOC recommended at $\ge 32\text{K}$ tokens for retrieval quality (and required above the $\sim 100\text{K}$-token VRAM crossover against Constant-$k=2$ RAG) with zero TOC prefix drift ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$); (2)~\textbf{In-Place Convex Sticky-Note Delta-Updates} with pre-gate RMS alignment on $\ell=2$; and (3)~\textbf{Hybrid Continuous-Discrete Page Memory}, where an $8\,\text{byte/token}$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} supports both $O(1)$ per-token embedding table lookup and parameter-free in-VRAM late-interaction \texttt{MaxSim} routing ($0\,\text{B}$ additional CPU lexical-index storage). Across $32\text{K}$--$256\text{K}$ tokens on frozen \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}, 2-level LSLC cuts live CUDA net VRAM by $1.70\times$--$3.58\times$ below Constant-$k=2$ Raw-Text RAG ($16.17$--$34.55\,\text{MiB}$ vs.\ $57.89$--$58.88\,\text{MiB}$, or $221\times$--$830\times$ below Full BF16 KV-cache). Under fair canonical first-sentence extraction across multi-seed $32\text{K}$ ($N=180$) and $128\text{K}$ ($N=60$) NIAH benchmarks, LSLC trades a bounded fraction of long-context retrieval ceiling against Raw-Text RAG ($80.0\%$ vs.\ $93.3\%$ Exact Extracted EM at $32\text{K}$; $70.0\%$ vs.\ $90.0\%$ at $128\text{K}$) in exchange for $8.25$--$8.85\,\text{ms/edit}$ in-place state revisions ($4.5\,\text{ms}$ zero-copy inference slice write; $57\times$--$1{,}369\times$ faster than LLM rewrites), achieving $94.4\%$--$95.8\%$ Extracted EM on structured $k=3$ numeric overrides (vs.\ $52.8\%$ for best RAG) with $97.2\%$ static $\ell=0$ policy retention, while highlighting that mutable text RAG still leads on $k=5$ symbolic overrides ($90.0\%$ vs.\ $40.0\%$--$48.3\%$) and simplest append-errata RAG edges out LSLC on paraphrased natural-language edits ($40.0\%$ vs.\ $30.0\%$--$35.0\%$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autoregressive Transformers store past context as per-layer key and value tensors growing linearly as $O(N \cdot d_{\text{kv}})$ with sequence length $N$. Even with modern Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) backbones such as \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} ($112\,\text{KiB/token}$ in FP16) and \texttt{Qwen3-4B} ($144\,\text{KiB/token}$ in FP16)~\cite{yang2025qwen3}, a $32{,}768$-token conversation consumes $3.50\,\text{GiB}$ to $4.50\,\text{GiB}$ of high-bandwidth VRAM per request. Worse, every decoding step streams the entire KV-cache from HBM, turning long-context generation into a memory-bandwidth bottleneck, while any revision to an earlier user constraint invalidates downstream KV states and forces costly suffix re-prefill.

Conceptually, a 1D KV-cache behaves like a $10\,\text{km}$ stone scroll: flat, sequential, memory-bound to scan, and rigid to edit. In contrast, human knowledge systems solve long-horizon memory and selective attention using a \textbf{book}: modular pages ($P$), a persistent global Table of Contents (TOC) enabling coarse-to-fine zoom, thematic strata separating durable foundations from margin sticky-notes, and a compact back-of-book index for exact lookups.

Existing alternatives each capture only fragments of this structure:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{KV-Cache Quantization and Eviction:} INT4 quantization cuts footprint by at most $4\times$, while eviction policies such as \textbf{H2O}~\cite{zhang2023h2o} and \textbf{StreamingLLM}~\cite{xiao2024streamingllm} permanently discard dropped tokens, leaving the model blind to evicted details if query focus shifts later.
    \item \textbf{Optical Text-as-Image Compression:} Methods like \textbf{DeepSeek-OCR}~\cite{deepseek2025ocr} and \textbf{Glyph}~\cite{glyph2025} render text onto 2D image canvases to exploit VLM visual compression. However, encoding RGB pixels wastes representational bandwidth on font glyphs and whitespace, requires heavy vision towers, collapses exact character fidelity above $10\times$--$20\times$ compression, and produces static pages that must be re-rendered upon edits.
    \item \textbf{1D Latent and Read-Only Hierarchical Memory:} Soft-prompt compressors (\textbf{ICAE}~\cite{ge2024icae}, \textbf{AutoCompressors}~\cite{chevalier2023autocompressors}) and recent 2026 hierarchical frameworks (\textbf{LCLMs}~\cite{li2026lclm}, \textbf{SeKV}~\cite{abaskohi2026sekv}, \textbf{GroupKV}~\cite{wang2026groupkv}, \textbf{LeanSparse}~\cite{pan2026leansparse}, and \textbf{Memorilla}~\cite{fallahpour2026memorilla}) bypass pixels and support coarse skimming, host-offloaded indexing, or chunk-level latent retrieval. However, existing latent stores (such as Memorilla~\cite{fallahpour2026memorilla}) rely on flat 1D chunk representations without disentangled 3D spatial-layer structure $[\ell, h, w]$ or an exact verbatim bypass, while recent editable KV-cache methods (\emph{Models Take Notes at Prefill}~\cite{li2026notes}) operate over uncompressed standard KV caches via append-only errata rather than in-place delta-updates over a 3D latent book ($132\times$--$830\times$ smaller net GPU VRAM than uncompressed Full BF16 KV-cache).
\end{enumerate}

We propose \textbf{Layered Spatial Latent Caching (LSLC)}, which directly compiles raw token streams into a compact, multi-layer 2D latent book $\vect{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times P \times L \times C \times H \times W}$ plus a sparse exact-token footnote bank, keeping the base LLM completely frozen. Our core contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Book-Structured Multi-Resolution Latent Tensor:} Each $T_p=256$-token chunk maps onto a $[L=3, C=128, H=4, W=4]$ spatial grid ($12\,\text{KiB/page}$ in FP16) trained with Matryoshka multi-scale supervision so a single tensor supports $1\times 1$ TOC ($1\,\text{token/page}$), $2\times 2$ Skim ($4\,\text{tokens/page}$), and layer-ordered Deep Read ($24\,\text{tokens/page}$).
    \item \textbf{Anti-Collapse Semantic Layer Disentanglement \& In-Place Delta-Updates:} Asymmetric write gating and layer-ordered causal projection separate static rules ($\ell=0$), entity state ($\ell=1$), and sparse-gated dynamic sticky-notes ($\ell=2$), enabling local turn revisions without re-encoding history while structurally preserving the LLM's TOC prefix KV-cache ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$).
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Bottleneck Exact-Recall Bypass:} A salience/surprisal gate routes $\sim 6.25\%$ verbatim high-entropy tokens to an $8\,\text{byte/token}$ sparse footnote bank resolved via $O(1)$ per-token embedding table lookup from the frozen LLM upon page Deep Read, overcoming the discrete representation-alignment bottleneck of continuous soft-prompt projections.
    \item \textbf{Exact End-to-End Memory Accounting \& Empirical Ablation Suite:} We verify net VRAM and TTFT bounds including both latent book storage and projected LLM active KV-cache on pure GQA transformers (\texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and \texttt{Qwen3-4B}), evaluating LSLC empirically against FP16 KV, INT4 KV, StreamingLLM, H2O, 1D flat/3-layer latent baselines, and 5-way mutable Raw-Text RAG across $1\text{K}$--$256\text{K}$ benchmarks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{KV-Cache Eviction and Hierarchical Inference.}
StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2024streamingllm} stabilizes infinite generation by retaining attention sinks plus a sliding local window, while H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o} preserves heavy-hitter tokens accumulating high attention scores. Both approaches suffer irreversible information loss under aggressive eviction. \textbf{SeKV}~\cite{abaskohi2026sekv} eliminates permanent eviction via entropy-segmented GPU summary vectors and host-offloaded low-rank SVD bases reconstructed on demand. Concurrent late-2026 systems extend coarse-to-fine KV management across specialized architectures: \textbf{GroupKV}~\cite{wang2026groupkv} introduces group-level summary indexing and staleness repair for diffusion LLMs, while \textbf{LeanSparse}~\cite{pan2026leansparse} pairs layer-wise prefill streaming with coarse-to-fine decode indexing and host RAM offloading for Native Sparse Attention models. Unlike host-offloading or diffusion-specific managers, LSLC keeps the entire compressed book resident in GPU VRAM ($16.2$--$34.4\,\text{MiB}$ at $32\text{K}$ tokens across $1.7\text{B}$--$4\text{B}$ backbones) for standard autoregressive GQA Transformers. Along the low-rank KV projection axis, \textbf{SALS}~\cite{mu2025sals} observes that RoPE inflates key-vector rank and therefore performs RoPE-free sparse token selection inside a low-rank latent space before reconstructing selected token KV states ($6.4\times$ compression). Whereas SALS targets read-only low-rank reconstruction of standard token KV caches, LSLC compiles raw pages into a 3D multi-resolution book ($132\times$--$830\times$ smaller net GPU VRAM than Full BF16 KV-cache) supporting $O(1)$ in-place state revisions.

\paragraph{Latent Context Compression and Optical Rendering.}
AutoCompressors~\cite{chevalier2023autocompressors} and ICAE~\cite{ge2024icae} compress spans into 1D continuous memory slots consumed as soft prompts by a frozen decoder. \textbf{LCLMs}~\cite{li2026lclm} scale encoder-decoder latent compression to $>350\text{B}$ pretraining tokens, enabling agents to skim and expand compressed reasoning. Most recently, \textbf{Memorilla}~\cite{fallahpour2026memorilla} decouples persistent document memory from the frozen LLM context window by encoding text chunks once into a persistent latent store and projecting query-conditioned soft memory tokens at inference time. Along the orthogonal multi-agent communication axis, \textbf{XKV}~\cite{liu2026xkv} translates KV caches across heterogeneous frozen LLMs via dual-cache latent pooling and cross-layer mixing to avoid text-decoding overhead between collaborating agents. However, as our Track~0E and iso-budget 1D-vs-3D ablations confirm, flat 1D latent soft-tokens struggle on exact verbatim identifier extraction and lack 2D multi-resolution spatial pooling or structural layer disentanglement for prefix invariance under edits. Orthogonal visual approaches---DeepSeek-OCR~\cite{deepseek2025ocr} and Glyph~\cite{glyph2025}---render text into images for 2D VLM compression, incurring glyph overhead and degraded verbatim OCR accuracy at $20\times$ ratios. LSLC retains the 2D multi-resolution inductive bias via spatial pooling and 3D rotary geometry~\cite{su2024roformer} while avoiding RGB rendering entirely and pairing the 3D latent book with an $8\,\text{byte/token}$ \texttt{SparseExactBank}.

\paragraph{Mutable Neural Memory, Editable KV Caches, and Looped State Refinement.}
Test-time memory architectures such as \textbf{Titans}~\cite{behrouz2025titans} learn to update neural long-term memory weights online during inference, whereas Perceiver-style cross-attention architectures~\cite{jaegle2021perceiver} map arbitrary inputs onto fixed latent arrays. Closest to our editability motivation, Li~\cite{li2026notes} (\emph{Models Take Notes at Prefill}) shows that standard KV caches act as memoized prefill conclusions that can be amended via append-only CoT errata while maintaining high vLLM prefix-cache hit rates. Concurrently along the orthogonal axis of recurrent compute depth, \textbf{GLM-5.4}~\cite{zai2026glm54} (\emph{Loop Transformer}) demonstrates at $750\text{B}$-parameter scale that multi-pass hidden-state overwrites suffer severe activation-norm explosion unless governed by independent \emph{gated progressive updates} (conservative global context formation in Pass~1 followed by difficulty-sensitive local refinement in Pass~2), paired with a \emph{hybrid KV cache} that reuses Pass~1 sparse KV states and attention indices alongside a compact Pass~2 local window. LSLC exhibits a conceptual similarity in gated residual updates and prefix cache reuse along the \emph{context-memory axis}: executing \emph{in-place, $O(1)$-memory convex spatial delta overrides} ($\vect{Z}_{p,2} \leftarrow (1-\vect{G}_p)\odot\vect{Z}_{p,2} + \vect{G}_p\odot\Delta\vect{Z}_{p,2}$) inside a 3D latent book ($132\times$--$221\times$ smaller net VRAM than Full BF16 KV at $32\text{K}$) while reusing a write-protected global prefix ($\text{TOC}_{1..P}$ and $\ell \in \{0,1\}$, $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0$) and sharing router \texttt{deep\_idx} indices across latent projection and $O(1)$ per-token \texttt{SparseExactBank} embedding lookup.

Let an incoming token sequence $X = (x_1, \dots, x_N)$ be partitioned into modular pages of length $T_p = 256$ tokens, yielding $P = \lceil N / T_p \rceil$ pages with frozen token embeddings $\vect{E}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{T_p \times d_{\text{llm}}}$. LSLC encodes each page independently into a multi-layer 2D latent tensor $\vect{Z}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times C \times H \times W}$ ($L=3, C=128, H=4, W=4$) plus a sparse exact index $\mathcal{I}_p$, and decodes context dynamically on demand for a frozen LLM backbone (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).

\subsection{Pillar 1: Spatial-Layer Encoder \& 3D Factorized RoPE}
Inside \texttt{LayeredLatentEncoder}, the $T_p=256$ tokens of page $p$ are divided into $H \times W = 16$ ordered $16$-token discourse sub-spans so spatial neighborhoods $(h, w)$ preserve local sequential continuity under 2D pooling. A lightweight Perceiver-style cross-attention stack~\cite{jaegle2021perceiver} maps learnable queries $\vect{Q}_{\text{book}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(L \cdot H \cdot W) \times d_{\text{enc}}}$ over $\vect{E}_p$, followed by bottleneck projection to $C=128$ channels.

To encode layer identity alongside 2D spatial layout without confounding dimensions, internal encoder and delta-updater self-attention applies \textbf{3D Factorized Spatial-Layer RoPE}~\cite{su2024roformer}. Partitioning the rotary channels $d_{\text{rot}}$ into $(d_\ell, d_h, d_w)$, each latent slot at coordinate $(\ell, h, w)$ rotates its query/key sub-vectors by:
\begin{equation}
\vect{R}(\ell, h, w) = \mathrm{diag}\big(\vect{R}_{d_\ell}(\ell),\; \vect{R}_{d_h}(h),\; \vect{R}_{d_w}(w)\big),
\end{equation}
while cross-page routing uses a separate 1D page-index rotary offset $p \in \{0, \dots, P-1\}$ so pages remain modular and appendable without re-indexing prior pages.

\subsection{Pillar 2: Multi-Resolution Book Pyramid \& Matryoshka Zoom}
Given a page tensor $\vect{Z}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times C \times 4 \times 4}$, LSLC constructs three hierarchical reading resolutions using $O(L \cdot C \cdot H \cdot W)$ pooling:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Level 0 --- Table of Contents (TOC, $1\times 1$):} Global average pooling over the consolidated structural layers $\vect{Z}_{p, 0:2}$ ($\ell \in \{0,1\}$, $2C=256$ channels) produces $\vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}} = \mathrm{Proj}_{\text{TOC}}(\mathrm{Pool}_{1\times 1}(\vect{Z}_{p, 0:2})) \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_{\text{llm}}}$, emitting \textbf{$1$ token/page} permanently resident in LLM context while staying invariant under $\ell=2$ delta-updates.
    \item \textbf{Level 1 --- Skimming ($2\times 2$):} Stride-$2$ average pooling over $\vect{Z}_{p, 0:2}$ produces $\vect{U}_p^{\text{Skim}} \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times d_{\text{llm}}}$ (\textbf{$4$ tokens/page}) for moderately relevant pages.
    \item \textbf{Level 2 --- Deep Read (Layer-Ordered):} Preserves spatial structure with asymmetric per-layer resolution: $2\times 2$ ($4$ tokens) for Static ($\ell=0$), $2\times 2$ ($4$ tokens) for Entity ($\ell=1$), and full $4\times 4$ ($16$ tokens) for Dynamic working memory ($\ell=2$), totaling \textbf{$24$ dense tokens/page}.
\end{itemize}

At inference time, \texttt{QueryToTOCRouter} scores a user query $X_q = (q_1, \dots, q_M)$ (with mean embedding $\vect{q}$) against page $p \in \{1, \dots, P\}$ by combining dense \texttt{PageRoPE1D}-rotated TOC similarity with parameter-free in-VRAM late-interaction \texttt{MaxSim} over the page's $K_{\text{ex}}=16$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} token IDs $\mathcal{I}_p$ (resolved via $O(1)$ per-token lookup from the frozen LLM embedding table $\vect{e}(\cdot)$ with $0\,\text{B}$ additional CPU lexical-index storage):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_p &= \frac{(\vect{W}_q \vect{q})^\top (\vect{W}_k \vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}})}{\sqrt{d_r}} \\
&\quad + \alpha_{\text{sal}}\,\mathrm{zscore}_p\!\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}^*|}\sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}^*} \max_{\vect{v} \in \mathcal{V}_p} \cos\big(\vect{e}(q_m), \vect{v}\big)\bigg),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{V}_p = \{\vect{e}(\mathcal{I}_{p,j})\}_{j=1}^{K_{\text{ex}}} \cup \{\vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}}\}$ and $\mathcal{M}^* \subset \{1, \dots, M\}$ selects the top-$4$ highest-affinity query tokens, routing top-$k_{\text{deep}}$ pages ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{deep}}$) to Deep Read, next-$k_{\text{skim}}$ pages ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{skim}}$) to Skim, and remaining pages to TOC. (Optionally, an external lexical retriever such as Okapi BM25 can also override routing indices via \texttt{router\_out\_override}.) For a $16$-page ($4{,}096$-token) book ($k_{\text{deep}}=2, k_{\text{skim}}=2, 12$ TOC pages) plus $32$ exact-index tokens, active LLM tokens drop to $12 + 8 + 48 + 32 = 100$. For a $128$-page ($32{,}768$-token) book ($k_{\text{deep}}=2, k_{\text{skim}}=4, 122$ flat TOC pages), active tokens total $122 + 16 + 48 + 32 = 218$.

\paragraph{2-Level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page Hierarchical TOC (Recommended Default at $\ge 32\text{K}$ Tokens).}
To prevent active TOC KV tokens from growing linearly as $O(P)$---which both dilutes self-attention over Deep Read tokens starting at $32\text{K}$ ($P=128$) and crosses above Constant-$k=2$ RAG's $512$-token active KV memory at $P^* \approx 384.3$ pages ($\approx 98.4\text{K}$ tokens)---LSLC partitions the $P$ pages into $C_{\text{chap}} = \lceil P / c \rceil$ contiguous chapters $\mathcal{P}_m = \{mc, \dots, \min(P, (m+1)c)-1\}$ of size $c$ ($c=16$ pages $= 4{,}096$ tokens). Each chapter $m \in \{0, \dots, C_{\text{chap}}-1\}$ forms a 1-token Chapter-TOC via mean pooling over its constituent page TOC vectors, and expands individual Page-TOCs exclusively within the active chapters $\mathcal{C}_{\text{sel}} = \{\lfloor p / c \rfloor \mid p \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{deep}}\}$ containing routed Deep Read pages:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vect{u}_m^{\text{Chap}} &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_m|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_m} \vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_{\text{llm}}}, \\
\text{TOC}_{\text{2-lvl}} &= \Big[\vect{u}_0^{\text{Chap}}, \dots, \vect{u}_{C_{\text{chap}}-1}^{\text{Chap}} \;\Big| \\
&\quad\;\; \big\{\vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}} : p \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{sel}} \setminus (\mathcal{D}_{\text{deep}} \cup \mathcal{D}_{\text{skim}})\big\}\Big],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}_{\text{sel}} = \bigcup_{m \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{sel}}} \mathcal{P}_m$. This bounds active TOC tokens by $\lceil P/c \rceil + k_{\text{deep}}(c-1)$ (shrinking the $32\text{K}$ active prefix from $218$ to $118$--$133$ tokens, $128\text{K}$ from $602$ to $157$--$158$, and $256\text{K}$ from $1{,}114$ to $189$) while strictly preserving $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0$ because every $\vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}}$ pools exclusively over write-protected layers $\ell \in \{0,1\}$.

To prevent blurred zoom-out representations when pooling $4\times 4 \rightarrow 2\times 2 \rightarrow 1\times 1$, training enforces \textbf{Matryoshka Multi-Scale Supervision}: stochastic resolution dropout samples TOC-only, Skim, or Deep Read projections during optimization so a single page tensor remains decodable across every zoom level.

\subsection{Pillar 3: Anti-Collapse Layers, Prefix Caching \& Delta-Updates}
Unconstrained end-to-end training causes \emph{layer collapse}, mixing static and transient features indiscriminately across $\ell \in \{0,1,2\}$. LSLC enforces structural specialization via two inductive biases:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layer-Ordered Two-Bank Causal Prefix Alignment (Mode~A):} Soft-prompt tokens enter the frozen LLM \texttt{inputs\_embeds} in strict monotonicity of volatility, forming a \textbf{Two-Bank Prefix} over a single causal attention softmax:
    \begin{align}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{LLM}} = \Big[&\underbrace{\text{TOC}_{1..P} \mid \text{Skim} \mid \text{Deep}_{\ell=0} \mid \text{Deep}_{\ell=1}}_{\text{Bank 1: Invariant Structural Prefix}} \nonumber \\
    &\mid \underbrace{\text{Deep}_{\ell=2} \mid \mathcal{I}_{\text{deep}}}_{\text{Bank 2: Mutable}} \mid X_{\text{turn}}\Big].
    \end{align}
    Whenever a turn revision modifies only $\ell=2$, the LLM's KV prefix cache for the global $\text{TOC}_{1..P}$ block remains $100\%$ invariant across all queries and edits by structural construction ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0$, since TOC pools exclusively over $\ell \in \{0,1\}$). Furthermore, whenever consecutive turns inspect the same routed page set (\texttt{deep\_idx}, \texttt{skim\_idx}), the entire Bank~1 prefix ($\text{TOC}_{1..P} \mid \text{Skim} \mid \text{Deep}_{\ell \in \{0,1\}}$) remains bit-identical ($\max|\Delta_{\text{Bank1}}| = 0.0$), updating only Bank~2 at the tail of the prefix (whereas if a dynamic edit shifts runner-up \texttt{deep\_idx} selection across pages, only the newly routed runner-up $\text{Skim}/\text{Deep}_{\ell \in \{0,1\}}$ slice refreshes behind the immutable TOC prefix). Notably, this prefix layout shares a conceptual similarity with the \emph{Two-Bank Sparse MLA} (\texttt{[Pass 1 Sparse Prefix | Pass 2 128-token Local SWA Window]}) introduced for recurrent layer looping in GLM-5.4~\cite{zai2026glm54}. (Mode~B replaces soft-prompt prefixes with gated cross-attention adapters~\cite{jaegle2021perceiver} in future work to eliminate causal ordering dependencies entirely.)
    \item \textbf{In-Place Sticky-Note Delta-Updates (Spatial Damp Gate with Pre-Gate RMS Alignment):} Given a revision turn $X_{\Delta}$ targeting page $p$ (selected by router top-$1$ or explicit page address), \texttt{DeltaUpdater} predicts a sparse update gate $\vect{G}_p \in [0, 1]^{H \times W}$ and raw override candidate $\widetilde{\Delta\vect{Z}}_{p,2} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$, aligns the candidate's channel-wise RMS norm to $\vect{Z}_{p,2}^{\text{old}}$ via $\Delta\vect{Z}_{p,2} = \widetilde{\Delta\vect{Z}}_{p,2} \odot \mathrm{clip}\!\big(\mathrm{RMS}(\vect{Z}_{p,2}^{\text{old}})/\mathrm{RMS}(\widetilde{\Delta\vect{Z}}_{p,2}),\, \frac{1}{4},\, 4\big)$, and applies the convex override exclusively to dynamic layer $\ell=2$:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:delta}
    \begin{aligned}
    \vect{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{new}} &= (1 - \vect{G}_p) \odot \vect{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{old}} + \vect{G}_p \odot \Delta\vect{Z}_{p, 2} \\
    &\equiv \vect{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{old}} + \vect{G}_p \odot \big(\Delta\vect{Z}_{p, 2} - \vect{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{old}}\big), \\
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}} &= \lambda_{\text{sp}} \Phi_{[\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]}(\|\vect{G}_p\|_1),
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    where $\Phi_{[\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]}$ penalizes gate activation outside target sparsity band $[0.12, 0.26]$, while structural layers $\vect{Z}_{p, 0:2}$ ($\ell \in \{0,1\}$) remain strictly write-protected after initial consolidation. Functionally, Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta} is analogous to the recurrent \emph{Damp Gate} ($\vect{x}_t = \vect{x}_{t-1} + \beta_t(\vect{h}_t - \vect{x}_{t-1})$) used in GLM-5.4~\cite{zai2026glm54} to prevent activation-norm explosion across multi-pass hidden-state overwrites.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pillar 4: Sparse Exact-Recall Footnote Bank \& Objective}
Although a $12\,\text{KiB}$ FP16 page latent ($6{,}144$ continuous scalars) occupies more raw bytes than $256$ discrete \texttt{int32} token IDs ($1\,\text{KiB}$), continuous soft-prompt vectors must project into distributed $d_{\text{llm}}$-dimensional hidden semantics rather than indexing the discrete vocabulary table directly. Consequently, pure continuous bottlenecks suffer an optimization and representation-alignment bottleneck on arbitrary high-entropy identifiers (hex hashes, random numeric codes, snake\_case symbols). To decouple semantic compression from discrete identifier retention without falling back to full-page raw-text re-prefill (which would consume $256$ active KV tokens per retrieved page rather than $24 + 16 = 40$), \texttt{SalienceGate} scores every raw token $i$ in page $p$ via:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_i &= \sigma\big(\vect{w}_s^\top \vect{e}_i + \gamma H_{\text{surprisal}}(x_i) \\
&\quad + \beta_{\text{code}}\mathbb{I}_{\text{code}}(i) + \beta_{\text{hdr}}\mathbb{I}_{\text{hdr}}(i)\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
combining learned projection, rare-token surprisal $H_{\text{surprisal}}(x_i)$, dense numeric/identifier span detection $\mathbb{I}_{\text{code}}(i)$, and a structural right-of-header window $\mathbb{I}_{\text{hdr}}(i)$ ($12$ tokens following \texttt{`:'} or \texttt{`]'
} delimiters to capture static $\ell=0$ policy values alongside exact codes), selecting the top-$\rho$ ($\rho = 6.25\%$, i.e., $16$ tokens per $256$-token page) into compact $8$-byte records $(\texttt{token\_id}\!:\!\texttt{int32},\allowbreak\;\texttt{page\_id}\!:\!\texttt{uint16},\allowbreak\;\texttt{h}\!:\!\texttt{uint8},\allowbreak\;\texttt{w}\!:\!\texttt{uint8})$ consuming only $128\,\text{bytes/page}$ ($16\,\text{KiB}$ for $32{,}768$ tokens). When page $p$ enters Deep Read, embeddings are retrieved via $O(1)$ per-token lookup from the frozen LLM's \texttt{embed\_tokens} table, summed with bounded $(h, w)$ spatial tags, and weighted by relative router confidence $(p_{\text{deep}}/p_{\max})^2$ prior to $X_{\text{turn}}$ (supplying exact token embeddings to self-attention, while final token emission remains governed by autoregressive decoding).

All trainable LSLC modules ($40.42\text{M}$ trainable parameters on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and $53.52\text{M}$ on \texttt{Qwen3-4B}; base LLM strictly frozen) are optimized jointly via:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{NTP}} + \lambda_{\text{rec}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{autoenc}} + \lambda_{\text{mat}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Matryoshka}} \\
&\quad + \lambda_{\text{sp}}\|\vect{G}\|_1 + \lambda_{\text{route}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{router}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
combining next-token prediction, page autoencoding reconstruction, multi-scale zoom distillation, delta-gate $L_1$ sparsity, and auxiliary router supervision.

\subsection{Key Empirical Findings}
\paragraph{1.\ 3D Spatial-Layer Structure vs.\ 1D Flat Latents (Track~0E, Track~2 \& Iso-Budget Ablation).}
Holding the latent byte budget strictly equal at $12\,\text{KiB/page}$ ($24$ projected tokens/page), replacing LSLC's 3D $[\ell, h, w]$ grid and \texttt{FactorizedRoPE3D} with an unindexed 1D flat latent compressor (Track~0E, analogous to ICAE/Memorilla chunk slots) degrades WikiText-2 perplexity from $\mathbf{15.66}$ to $24.05$ ($+8.39$ PPL penalty), drops Semantic NIAH from $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ to $46.7\%$, and completely breaks prefix invariance when a page is edited ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 1.097$ vs.\ $\mathbf{0.0}$ in LSLC). Furthermore, our stored-parameter ($40.42\text{M}$), iso-byte ($12.125\,\text{KiB/page}$), iso-step, and iso-\texttt{SparseExactBank} ablation in Table~\ref{tab:iso_ablation} compares the complete \texttt{3D-Spatial} package against two 1D controls that simultaneously disable \texttt{FactorizedRoPE3D}, zero and freeze the $3\times 3$ \texttt{Conv2d} mixers, and replace the bottom-row sticky-note spatial prior with a uniform prior (and \texttt{1D-Flat} additionally averages across all three layers, so active capacity differs despite identical stored parameter counts): equipping \texttt{1D-Flat} ($[1, 128, 48]$) with \texttt{SparseExactBank} lifts its $1\text{K}$ Exact NIAH Substring EM to $73.3\%$, yet \texttt{1D-Flat} still fails TOC prefix invariance ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.754$), achieves only $66.7\%$ Semantic NIAH, and suffers $22.41$ PPL; adding 3-layer separation without 2D spatial mixing (\texttt{1D-3Layer}, $[3, 128, 16]$) restores $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$ and lifts Semantic NIAH Substring EM to $93.3\%$ ($24.86$ PPL, $62.5\%$ static $\ell=0$ retention); and the full \texttt{3D-Spatial} package ($[3, 128, 4, 4]$ + \texttt{FactorizedRoPE3D} + 2D \texttt{Conv2d} + spatial prior) reduces WikiText-2 perplexity to $\mathbf{18.94}$ ($-3.47$ to $-5.92$ PPL points), reaches $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ Semantic NIAH Substring EM, and achieves $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ static $\ell=0$ retention with $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=\mathbf{0.0}$. (Isolating \texttt{FactorizedRoPE3D} alone from 2D \texttt{Conv2d} and the spatial prior remains for future ablation.)

\paragraph{2.\ Information-Theoretic Rescue via 8-Byte \texttt{SparseExactBank} (Track~1 vs.\ Track~2).}
On continuous 3D latents alone (Track~1), both backbones achieve $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ Semantic NIAH EM (surpassing StreamingLLM at $53.3\%$/$6.7\%$ and H2O-$20\%$ at $53.3\%$/$0.0\%$), confirming zero semantic context loss across all needle depths. However, continuous $C=128$ bottlenecks achieve $0.0\%$ Exact NIAH string match ($24.7\%$--$27.3\%$ token accuracy) on 9-token random numeric codes. Activating the $8\,\text{B/token}$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} ($128\,\text{B/page}$, Track~2) achieves $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ needle token capture rate, boosting teacher-forced Exact NIAH token accuracy to $\mathbf{90.7\%}$ on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} (matching Full KV's $90.0\%$) and lifting autoregressive Exact String Match by $\mathbf{+66.7}$ percentage points ($0.0\% \rightarrow \mathbf{66.7\%}$) on both \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and \texttt{Qwen3-4B}.

\paragraph{3.\ Positive Scaling Law \& Sparse-Footnote Fluency Trade-Off (\texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{Qwen3-4B}).}
Scaling the frozen backbone from $1.7\text{B}$ to $4\text{B}$ improves LSLC Track~1 perplexity from $16.59 \rightarrow \mathbf{12.88}$ (outperforming both StreamingLLM at $13.64$ and H2O-$20\%$ at $14.27$) and Track~2 perplexity from $15.66 \rightarrow \mathbf{14.24}$ (with pure \texttt{deep\_only} zoom reaching $\mathbf{13.51}$ and \texttt{toc\_only} reaching $15.78$). Notably, whereas adding \texttt{SparseExactBank} on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} (300 steps, $40.42\text{M}$ params) reduces mixed-mode PPL ($16.59 \rightarrow 15.66$), on the wider \texttt{Qwen3-4B} projector ($d_{\text{llm}}=2560$, $53.52\text{M}$ params trained under a fixed 250-step budget) injecting discrete verbatim footnote tokens introduces a modest general-prose fluency trade-off in mixed shallow/deep mode ($12.88 \rightarrow 14.24$ PPL, while \texttt{deep\_only} remains at $13.51$) alongside a slightly lower teacher-forced Exact NIAH token accuracy ($79.3\%$ vs.\ $90.7\%$ on $1.7\text{B}$), even as autoregressive Exact String Match ($66.7\%$) and needle capture ($100.0\%$) match $1.7\text{B}$. At $N=32{,}768$ tokens on \texttt{Qwen3-4B}, Full BF16 KV-cache requires $4{,}610.25\,\text{MiB}$ and $12{,}725.77\,\text{ms}$ ($12.73\,\text{s}$) TTFT, whereas LSLC consumes only $\mathbf{34.42\,\text{MiB}}$ ($\mathbf{133.93\times}$ smaller) with $\mathbf{111.83\,\text{ms}}$ Cold TTFT ($\mathbf{113.8\times}$ faster) and $\mathbf{84.30\,\text{ms}}$ Warm TTFT ($\mathbf{151.0\times}$ faster).

\section{Discussion, Error Decomposition \& Limitations}

\paragraph{3-Stage Error Decomposition on Multi-Page 4K Books (Track~3).}
On 16-page ($4{,}096$-token) books (Track~3 on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}), LSLC maintains $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ \texttt{SalienceGate} needle capture rate and $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ Semantic NIAH EM, while autoregressive Exact NIAH EM settles at $50.0\%$ ($70.0\%$ token accuracy). Telemetry decomposes this gap into three transparent stages: (1)~\emph{Intra-Page Capture} remains lossless at $100.0\%$ because \texttt{SalienceGate} operates page-modularly ($T_p=256$); (2)~\emph{Top-$k_{\text{deep}}$ Query-to-TOC Routing} ($k_{\text{deep}}=2$ of $16$ pages $= 12.5\%$ budget) achieves $40.0\%$ top-2 recall after 300 steps of light training, bounding exact footnote gather whenever the target page falls outside \texttt{deep\_idx}; and (3)~\emph{Cross-Page Attention Dilution} across $100$ active virtual tokens (including $16$ exact tokens from the runner-up \texttt{deep\_idx} page containing background Wikipedia dates/numbers).

\paragraph{Single-Page Confidence Weighting vs.\ Multi-Hop Trade-Off \& Static $\ell=0$ Replay (Track~4).}
In Track~4 ($N_{\text{train}}=96$, $N_{\text{eval}}=24$ held-out episodes, $k=3$ sequential in-place overrides per episode), \texttt{DeltaUpdater} maintains active spatial gate sparsity inside the target band ($\text{mean}(\vect{G}_p) = 0.191$--$0.259 \in [0.05, 0.35]$), achieves $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ held-out Delta-Edit Exact Match on both \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and \texttt{Qwen3-4B}, and preserves strict bit-identical TOC prefix invariance ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = \mathbf{0.0}$). Two architectural trade-offs surfaced during Track~4 analysis. First, while pre-override latent probes on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} retain static $\ell=0$ rules ($100.0\%$ EM), optimizing \texttt{DeltaUpdater} exclusively on override turns without joint static-query replay shifts un-replayed static readout ($0.0\%$ EM in single-task Track~4) even though $\vect{Z}_{p,0:2}$ remains bit-identical ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$). Adding the right-of-header \texttt{SalienceGate} window, joint static $\ell=0$ replay ($33\%$ episode mix), and freezing \texttt{projector.toc\_proj} during multi-scale router tuning restores static $\ell=0$ retention in our unified checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:editable_rag}) to $\mathbf{97.2\%}$ Extracted EM on $k=3$ numeric overrides, $\mathbf{86.7\%}$--$\mathbf{90.0\%}$ on $k=5$ symbolic overrides, and $\mathbf{93.3\%}$--$\mathbf{96.7\%}$ on paraphrased natural-language overrides. \emph{Methodological caveat on static retention:} Paper~1's state-override benchmark places the static policy on fixed page~$0$ with a fixed target string (\texttt{``dual signoff''}), fixes overrides on page~$1$, and draws paraphrased edits from four shared templates across training and evaluation; thus, while structural tensor invariance ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$) holds by construction, testing against randomized static facts/locations and fully held-out paraphrase families remains necessary to rule out template memorization. Second, weighting footnotes by relative router confidence $(p_{\text{deep}}/p_{\max})^2$ suppresses distractor noise for single-page queries at the cost of attenuating secondary pages during equal-weight multi-hop synthesis, motivating independent sigmoid threshold gating in future extensions.

\paragraph{Adversarial Stress-Testing, $k=5$ Symbolic Overrides \& Constant-$k$ RAG Memory Crossover (Table~\ref{tab:hard_stress}).}
To test whether LSLC's editability and hybrid exact-recall mechanism survive beyond 4-page numeric templates, Table~\ref{tab:hard_stress} evaluates five stress regimes across \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} (\texttt{BF16}) and \texttt{Qwen3-8B} (loaded with 4-bit \texttt{NF4} weights and \texttt{BF16} runtime KV-cache on a single $22\,\text{GiB}$ L4 GPU). Pairing integer \texttt{PageRoPE1D} with quadratic runner-up confidence attenuation $(p_{\text{deep}}/p_{\max})^2$ suppresses distractor footnotes from secondary pages, reaching $\mathbf{90.0\%}$ episode EM ($\mathbf{96.0\%}$ token accuracy) on 8-page multi-file Python codebases (\textbf{EC-1}) on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ EM on \texttt{Qwen3-8B}, alongside $\mathbf{85.0\%}$ ($1.7\text{B}$) and $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ ($8\text{B}$) EM under four near-miss decoy keys (\textbf{EC-2}). On context-grounded multi-step reasoning (\textbf{EC-5}), \texttt{Qwen3-8B} reaches $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ accuracy matching Full KV. Under deep $k=5$ sequential symbolic overrides (\textbf{EC-3}), a cold-trained \texttt{DeltaUpdater} scores $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ EM ($93.75\%$ token accuracy) on \texttt{BF16} \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and $55.0\%$ EM ($90.89\%$ token accuracy) on 4-bit \texttt{Qwen3-8B} due to cumulative pre-gate norm drift without an intermediate \texttt{RMSNorm}~\cite{zai2026glm54}.

Finally, live CUDA systems profiling from $32\text{K}$ to $256\text{K}$ tokens (Figure~\ref{fig:vram_crossover}) exposes both LSLC's Full-KV asymptote and its exact crossover against \textbf{Constant-$k$ Raw-Text Chunk RAG}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Against Full BF16 KV, a flat 1-level TOC emits $1$ active KV token per page alongside $12.125\,\text{KiB/page}$ storage, bounding 1-level compression as $P \to \infty$ by $\lim_{P\to\infty}\frac{256\cdot\text{KV}_{\text{tok}}}{12.125\,\text{KiB}+\text{KV}_{\text{tok}}} = \mathbf{231.0\times}$ ($1.7\text{B}$) and $\mathbf{236.1\times}$ ($4\text{B}/8\text{B}$).
    \item Standard \textbf{Constant-$k=2$ Raw-Text Chunk RAG} (zero TOC tokens in LLM KV; strictly $512$ retrieved raw tokens $= 57.89\text{--}58.88\,\text{MiB}$ live on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}, storing $1{,}152\,\text{B/page}$ raw IDs+index) keeps active KV constant, whereas flat 1-level LSLC grows by $127{,}104\,\text{B/page}$ ($1.7\text{B}$), crossing at:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    P^* &= \frac{(512 - 90)\cdot \text{KV}_{\text{tok}}}{12{,}416 + \text{KV}_{\text{tok}} - 1{,}152} \\
    &\Longrightarrow \mathbf{384.3\text{ pages} \;(\approx 98.4\text{K})} \text{ on } \texttt{1.7B}, \\
    &\hphantom{\Longrightarrow\;} \mathbf{392.1\text{ pages} \;(\approx 100.4\text{K})} \text{ on } \texttt{4B/8B}.
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    At $128\text{K}$ ($73.66\,\text{MiB}$) and $256\text{K}$ ($135.72\,\text{MiB}$), flat 1-level LSLC indeed exceeds Constant-$k=2$ RAG ($58.31$--$58.88\,\text{MiB}$ GPU VRAM plus $0.26$--$0.84\,\text{MiB}$ CPU BM25 host RAM). Activating our \textbf{2-level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page hierarchy} (\texttt{chapter\_size=16}) cuts live CUDA net VRAM on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} to $\mathbf{16.17\text{--}17.81\,\text{MiB}}$ ($32\text{K}$, $118\text{--}133$ active prefix tokens depending on whether top-$2$ pages share a chapter, $3.25\times$--$3.58\times <$ RAG), $\mathbf{24.98\text{--}25.09\,\text{MiB}}$ ($128\text{K}$, $157\text{--}158$ tokens, $2.32\times$--$2.33\times <$ RAG), and $\mathbf{34.55\,\text{MiB}}$ ($256\text{K}$, $189$ tokens, $1.70\times <$ RAG; $829.9\times <$ Full BF16 KV), while maintaining $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Unified Multi-Seed Evaluation, Pure GPU vs.\ Hybrid Routing, Equal-Token TOC Control \& 5-Way Editable RAG Frontier (Tables~\ref{tab:retrieval_v2}--\ref{tab:iso_ablation}).}
To evaluate retrieval and editing under identical greedy decoding ($24$ max new tokens, stopping only on EOS or a period/newline after non-whitespace output) and deterministic first-sentence canonical extraction (\texttt{v2.1.0}), Tables~\ref{tab:retrieval_v2}--\ref{tab:iso_ablation} benchmark our unified \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} checkpoint across three seeds ($\{42, 1337, 9999\}$, $N=180$ $32\text{K}$ NIAH, $N=60$ $128\text{K}$ NIAH, and $N=288$ state-override episodes on NVIDIA L4 \texttt{BF16}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{End-to-End Pure GPU Routing ($0\,\text{B}$ additional CPU index) vs.\ Hybrid Shared-BM25 \& Long-Context Ceiling (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval_v2}):} Under fair canonical extraction, Raw-Text Chunk RAG achieves $\mathbf{93.3\%}$ Exact Extracted EM at $32\text{K}$ and $\mathbf{90.0\%}$ Exact / $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ Semantic Substring EM at $128\text{K}$ ($13.3\%$--$16.7\%$ Clause/Prefix EM solely because raw Wikipedia contexts elicit conversational preambles before the code, confirming why Extracted EM is the fairer cross-system headline metric). With $0\,\text{B}$ additional CPU lexical storage, 2-Level End-to-End LSLC achieves $\mathbf{61.1\%}$ Exact and $\mathbf{77.8\%}$ Semantic Extracted EM ($98.9\%$ Substring) at $32\text{K}$, and $\mathbf{56.7\%}$ Exact / $\mathbf{43.3\%}$ Semantic Extracted EM ($73.3\%$ Substring) at $128\text{K}$. Pairing LSLC with shared CPU Okapi BM25 ($270$--$857\,\text{KiB}$ host RAM) lifts Exact Extracted EM to $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ at $32\text{K}$ ($97.0\%$ content-tok acc; $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ Semantic Substring EM) and $\mathbf{70.0\%}$ at $128\text{K}$ ($94.1\%$ content-tok acc). Thus, LSLC explicitly trades a bounded fraction of long-context retrieval ceiling against Raw-Text RAG ($-13.3\text{ pts}$ Exact at $32\text{K}$; $-20.0\text{ pts}$ Exact and $-30.0\text{ pts}$ Semantic Substring at $128\text{K}$) in exchange for $2.32\times$--$3.58\times$ smaller live CUDA VRAM and $O(1)$-VRAM in-place mutability.
    \item \textbf{Decomposing 2-Level Hierarchical TOC via Equal-Token Truncated Flat Control ($133$/$158$ Tokens):} Comparing \texttt{LSLC (Shared BM25, Flat 1-Level TOC)} ($218$ tokens at $32\text{K}$; $602$ at $128\text{K}$) against an equal-token \texttt{Truncated Flat TOC Control} ($133$ tokens at $32\text{K}$; $158$ at $128\text{K}$) demonstrates that \textbf{pruning active TOC tokens}---eliminating self-attention dilution over Deep Read tokens---drives virtually the entire quality recovery starting at $32\text{K}$ ($5.6\% \rightarrow 75.6\%$ $32\text{K}$ Semantic Extracted EM; $13.3\% \rightarrow 33.3\%$ at $128\text{K}$). At equal token budget, 2-level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page pooling ($c=16$) matches Truncated Flat on Exact NIAH ($\Delta = 0.0\% \pm 0.0\%$) and $128\text{K}$ Semantic NIAH ($\Delta = 0.0\% \pm 0.0\%$), while adding a modest $+2.2\% \pm 2.2\%$ ($95\%$ seed CI; $\pm 4.4\%$ episode CI) Semantic Extracted EM edge at $32\text{K}$ and preserving coarse chapter summaries across $100\%$ of the book rather than dropping $70\%$--$86\%$ of pages from the global TOC.
    \item \textbf{5-Way Editable RAG Frontier \& Generalization Limits (Table~\ref{tab:editable_rag}):} On structured $k=3$ numeric overrides ($N=108$), LSLC \texttt{DeltaUpdater} ($\mathbf{94.4\%}$--$\mathbf{95.8\%}$ Extracted EM, $97.2\%$ static $\ell=0$ retention, $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$) substantially outperforms the strongest mutable text RAG baseline (\texttt{Append-Errata}, $52.8\%$; \texttt{In-Place LLM Rewrite}, $47.2\%$) at $8.85\,\text{ms/edit}$ ($55\times$--$1{,}276\times$ faster than LLM rewrites). However, on $k=5$ symbolic overrides ($N=90$), deterministic \texttt{Direct Text-Patch} ($1.15\,\text{ms}$) and \texttt{LLM Rewrite} ($504.7\,\text{ms}$) reach $\mathbf{90.0\%}$ Extracted EM versus LSLC's $40.0\%$--$48.3\%$. Moreover, on paraphrased natural-language edits ($N=90$), while \texttt{Direct Text-Patch} collapses to $0.0\%$ ($0\%$ regex match), the simplest \texttt{Append-Errata} baseline reaches $\mathbf{40.0\% \pm 8.7\%}$ Extracted EM vs.\ LSLC's $30.0\%$--$35.0\%$ (a 5-point difference equal to 3/60 override queries) and \texttt{LLM Rewrite} scores $30.0\%$ ($93.3\%$ TF tok acc). Taken together, these results establish LSLC's current sweet spot on structured numeric state updates while framing open-ended natural-language and multi-step symbolic editing---as well as stronger constrained-extractor RAG baselines---as open generalization limits.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Oracle A/B/C Diagnostic on $k=1 \dots 5$ Symbolic Overrides \& Book-Size Edit Scaling (\texttt{v21\_diagnostics}).}
To pinpoint where accuracy is lost on $k=5$ symbolic overrides ($N=30$, $\text{seed}=42$, oracle target page $1$ supplied directly to eliminate routing error), we compare: \textbf{Condition~A} (verified up-to-date raw text page), \textbf{Condition~B} (same verified page re-encoded from scratch by \texttt{LayeredLatentEncoder}), and \textbf{Condition~C} (initial book updated sequentially at $t=1 \dots 5$ by \texttt{DeltaUpdater}). Across $t=1 \dots 5$, Condition~A sustains $86.7\%$--$93.3\%$ Extracted EM ($90.0\%$ at $k=5$), whereas Condition~B scores $0.0\%$ Extracted EM ($25.9\%$--$34.0\%$ TF content-tok acc) and Condition~C fluctuates at $10.0\% \rightarrow 30.0\% \rightarrow 26.7\% \rightarrow 40.0\% \rightarrow 23.3\%$ ($70.6\%$--$84.4\%$ TF tok acc; $\bar{g}=0.201$--$0.250$). This decomposition proves that router error accounts for only $8.3\,\text{pts}$ ($40.0\% \rightarrow 48.3\%$ in Table~\ref{tab:editable_rag}); the dominant bottleneck lies in base encoder/projector generalization to unseen symbolic state schemas (Condition~B) compounded by multi-step \texttt{DeltaUpdater} instability (Condition~C). Separately, profiling \texttt{DeltaUpdater.update\_book\_page} across $P \in \{4, 128, 1024\}$ pages ($1\text{K}$--$256\text{K}$ tokens, known target page address) shows that the default autograd-safe \texttt{z\_book.clone()} maintains $7.56$--$7.72\,\text{ms/edit}$ latency while adding linear temporary peak CUDA allocation ($11.87\,\text{MiB}$ at $P=4 \rightarrow 23.83\,\text{MiB}$ at $P=1024$ due to cloning the $12.0\,\text{MiB}$ latent book), whereas zero-copy in-place slice assignment (\texttt{z\_book[:, p] = new\_page}) during inference reduces latency to $\mathbf{4.47}$--$\mathbf{4.51\,\text{ms/edit}}$ with $O(1)$ peak allocation delta ($\mathbf{11.58}$--$\mathbf{12.46\,\text{MiB}}$ across $1\text{K}$--$256\text{K}$).

\section{Conclusion}

We presented the architecture, byte-level systems accounting, and fair empirical evaluation of \textbf{Layered Spatial Latent Caching (LSLC)} across \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}, \texttt{Qwen3-4B}, and 4-bit \texttt{Qwen3-8B}. Combining a $[L=3, C=128, H=4, W=4]$ spatial-layer book with an $8\,\text{byte/token}$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} (supporting both $O(1)$ per-token embedding table lookup and $0\,\text{B}$ additional CPU lexical-index storage for in-VRAM \texttt{MaxSim} routing), volatility-ordered Two-Bank prefix caching ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$), RMS-aligned convex $\ell=2$ \texttt{DeltaUpdater} overrides, and a 2-level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page hierarchy (recommended from $\ge 32\text{K}$ for attention-dilution control and required above the $\sim 100\text{K}$ VRAM crossover vs.\ Constant-$k=2$ RAG) yields $1.70\times$--$3.58\times$ smaller live CUDA net VRAM than Constant-$k=2$ Raw-Text RAG across $32\text{K}$--$256\text{K}$ ($221\times$--$830\times$ below Full BF16 KV-cache). Under fair canonical extraction (\texttt{v2.1.0}), LSLC reaches $80.0\%$ Exact / $77.8\%$ Semantic Extracted EM ($100.0\%$ Substring) at $32\text{K}$ and $70.0\%$ Exact Extracted EM at $128\text{K}$---trading a bounded fraction of long-context retrieval ceiling against Raw-Text RAG ($93.3\%$ at $32\text{K}$; $90.0\%$ Exact / $100.0\%$ Semantic Substring at $128\text{K}$) for $8.25$--$8.85\,\text{ms/edit}$ ($4.5\,\text{ms}$ zero-copy) in-place updates that achieve $94.4\%$--$95.8\%$ Extracted EM on structured $k=3$ numeric overrides, while exposing concrete headroom on $k=5$ symbolic overrides, open-ended paraphrased edits, randomized static retention, and multi-file coding-agent workflows.

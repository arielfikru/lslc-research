\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) over raw text chunks avoids quadratic Full-KV prefill compute ($O(N^2)$) and linear KV-cache memory growth ($O(N \cdot d_{\text{kv}})$) by retrieving top-$k$ pages per query, yet remains brittle under multi-turn in-place revisions: immutable chunks serve stale constraints ($0.0\%$ override EM), appending errata leaves conflicting history in the prompt ($40.0\%$--$52.8\%$ Substring EM; $0.0\%$--$5.6\%$ Strict EM), deterministic regex text-patching ($1.33$--$1.41\,\text{ms/edit}$) requires rigid templates and collapses to $0.0\%$ EM on paraphrased natural-language revisions, and LLM-based chunk rewrites incur $496.4$--$11{,}250.9\,\text{ms/edit}$ on NVIDIA L4 ($790.9$--$14{,}630.9\,\text{ms/edit}$ on Tesla T4). Conversely, recent 2026 latent context compressors (\textbf{LCLMs}, \textbf{Memorilla}) compress history into read-only 1D continuous slots that lose exact verbatim identifiers, while editable KV methods (\emph{Models Take Notes at Prefill}) operate over uncompressed standard KV caches. We target the unoccupied intersection of \emph{in-place editable compressed memory} and \emph{prefix-cache preservation} with \textbf{Layered Spatial Latent Caching (LSLC)}, representing context as a modular, multi-layer 2D latent book $\vect{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times P \times L \times C \times H \times W}$ ($12\,\text{KiB/page}$) paired with a $128\,\text{byte/page}$ \textbf{Sparse Exact-Recall Footnote Bank}. LSLC introduces three core mechanisms: (1)~\textbf{Volatility-Ordered Prefix Caching} (with 1-level TOC at $\le 32\text{K}$ and 2-level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page hierarchy above $\sim 100\text{K}$), ordering projected blocks from immutable global TOC and \emph{Skim} tokens through static/entity layers ($\ell \in \{0,1\}$) to mutable dynamic working memory ($\ell=2$), guaranteeing zero TOC prefix drift ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$); (2)~\textbf{In-Place Convex Sticky-Note Delta-Updates} with pre-gate channel-wise RMS alignment, overwriting stale constraints on $\ell=2$ via $\vect{Z}_{p,2} \leftarrow (1 - \vect{G}_p) \odot \vect{Z}_{p,2} + \vect{G}_p \odot \Delta\vect{Z}_{p,2}$; and (3)~\textbf{Hybrid Continuous-Discrete Page Memory}, pairing 3D spatial-layer latents (\texttt{FactorizedRoPE3D}) with an $8\,\text{byte/token}$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} that enables both $O(1)$ exact-token copy and parameter-free in-VRAM late-interaction \texttt{MaxSim} routing ($0\,\text{B}$ CPU host RAM). Against Constant-$k=2$ Raw-Text Chunk RAG ($57.89$--$58.88\,\text{MiB}$ live GPU VRAM plus $0.26$--$0.84\,\text{MiB}$ CPU BM25 index), 2-level hierarchical LSLC cuts live CUDA net VRAM by $1.70\times$--$3.58\times$ across $32\text{K}$--$256\text{K}$ ($16.17$--$17.81\,\text{MiB}$ at $32\text{K}$, $24.98$--$25.09\,\text{MiB}$ at $128\text{K}$, $34.55\,\text{MiB}$ at $256\text{K}$---up to $829.9\times$ below Full BF16 KV). Across multi-seed $32\text{K}$ ($N=180$) and $128\text{K}$ ($N=60$) NIAH evaluations, 2-level LSLC with $100\%$ GPU-resident routing achieves $74.4\%$ Strict / $97.8\%$ Substring Semantic EM at $32\text{K}$ ($46.7\%$ Strict / $76.7\%$ Substring at $128\text{K}$) and $31.1\%$--$33.3\%$ Strict / $56.7\%$--$61.1\%$ Substring Exact EM, while pairing LSLC with a shared BM25 retriever lifts Exact Substring EM to $82.2\%$ at $32\text{K}$ ($97.0\%$ content-token accuracy) and $73.3\%$ at $128\text{K}$ ($94.1\%$ content-token accuracy). Across 3-seed 5-way state overrides ($N=288$), LSLC executes edits in $8.62$--$8.70\,\text{ms/edit}$ on L4 ($57\times$--$1{,}294\times$ faster than LLM rewrites), achieving $94.4\%$ Strict / $95.8\%$ Substring EM ($100.0\%$ content-token accuracy) on $k=3$ numeric overrides, $38.3\%$--$43.3\%$ Strict / $40.0\%$--$46.7\%$ Substring EM ($82.0\%$--$86.6\%$ content-token accuracy) on $k=5$ symbolic overrides, $35.0\%$--$40.0\%$ Substring EM on paraphrased natural-language edits, and $86.7\%$--$97.2\%$ static $\ell=0$ policy retention EM.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autoregressive Transformers store past context as per-layer key and value tensors growing linearly as $O(N \cdot d_{\text{kv}})$ with sequence length $N$. Even with modern Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) backbones such as \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} ($112\,\text{KiB/token}$ in FP16) and \texttt{Qwen3-4B} ($144\,\text{KiB/token}$ in FP16)~\cite{yang2025qwen3}, a $32{,}768$-token conversation consumes $3.50\,\text{GiB}$ to $4.50\,\text{GiB}$ of high-bandwidth VRAM per request. Worse, every decoding step streams the entire KV-cache from HBM, turning long-context generation into a memory-bandwidth bottleneck, while any revision to an earlier user constraint invalidates downstream KV states and forces costly suffix re-prefill.

Conceptually, a 1D KV-cache behaves like a $10\,\text{km}$ stone scroll: flat, sequential, memory-bound to scan, and rigid to edit. In contrast, human knowledge systems solve long-horizon memory and selective attention using a \textbf{book}: modular pages ($P$), a persistent global Table of Contents (TOC) enabling coarse-to-fine zoom, thematic strata separating durable foundations from margin sticky-notes, and a compact back-of-book index for exact lookups.

Existing alternatives each capture only fragments of this structure:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{KV-Cache Quantization and Eviction:} INT4 quantization cuts footprint by at most $4\times$, while eviction policies such as \textbf{H2O}~\cite{zhang2023h2o} and \textbf{StreamingLLM}~\cite{xiao2024streamingllm} permanently discard dropped tokens, leaving the model blind to evicted details if query focus shifts later.
    \item \textbf{Optical Text-as-Image Compression:} Methods like \textbf{DeepSeek-OCR}~\cite{deepseek2025ocr} and \textbf{Glyph}~\cite{glyph2025} render text onto 2D image canvases to exploit VLM visual compression. However, encoding RGB pixels wastes representational bandwidth on font glyphs and whitespace, requires heavy vision towers, collapses exact character fidelity above $10\times$--$20\times$ compression, and produces static pages that must be re-rendered upon edits.
    \item \textbf{1D Latent and Read-Only Hierarchical Memory:} Soft-prompt compressors (\textbf{ICAE}~\cite{ge2024icae}, \textbf{AutoCompressors}~\cite{chevalier2023autocompressors}) and recent 2026 hierarchical frameworks (\textbf{LCLMs}~\cite{li2026lclm}, \textbf{SeKV}~\cite{abaskohi2026sekv}, \textbf{GroupKV}~\cite{wang2026groupkv}, \textbf{LeanSparse}~\cite{pan2026leansparse}, and \textbf{Memorilla}~\cite{fallahpour2026memorilla}) bypass pixels and support coarse skimming, host-offloaded indexing, or chunk-level latent retrieval. However, existing latent stores (such as Memorilla~\cite{fallahpour2026memorilla}) rely on flat 1D chunk representations without disentangled 3D spatial-layer structure $[\ell, h, w]$ or an exact verbatim bypass, while recent editable KV-cache methods (\emph{Models Take Notes at Prefill}~\cite{li2026notes}) operate over uncompressed standard KV caches via append-only errata rather than in-place delta-updates over a $132\times$-compressed 3D latent book.
\end{enumerate}

We propose \textbf{Layered Spatial Latent Caching (LSLC)}, which directly compiles raw token streams into a compact, multi-layer 2D latent book $\vect{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times P \times L \times C \times H \times W}$ plus a sparse exact-token footnote bank, keeping the base LLM completely frozen. Our core contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Book-Structured Multi-Resolution Latent Tensor:} Each $T_p=256$-token chunk maps onto a $[L=3, C=128, H=4, W=4]$ spatial grid ($12\,\text{KiB/page}$ in FP16) trained with Matryoshka multi-scale supervision so a single tensor supports $1\times 1$ TOC ($1\,\text{token/page}$), $2\times 2$ Skim ($4\,\text{tokens/page}$), and layer-ordered Deep Read ($24\,\text{tokens/page}$).
    \item \textbf{Anti-Collapse Semantic Layer Disentanglement \& In-Place Delta-Updates:} Asymmetric write gating and layer-ordered causal projection separate static rules ($\ell=0$), entity state ($\ell=1$), and sparse-gated dynamic sticky-notes ($\ell=2$), enabling local turn revisions without re-encoding history while structurally preserving the LLM's TOC prefix KV-cache ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$).
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Bottleneck Exact-Recall Bypass:} A salience/surprisal gate routes $\sim 6.25\%$ verbatim high-entropy tokens to an $8\,\text{byte/token}$ sparse footnote bank resolved in $O(1)$ via the frozen LLM embedding table upon page Deep Read, overcoming the discrete copy alignment bottleneck of continuous soft-prompt projections.
    \item \textbf{Exact End-to-End Memory Accounting \& Empirical Ablation Suite:} We verify net VRAM and TTFT bounds including both latent book storage and projected LLM active KV-cache on pure GQA transformers (\texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and \texttt{Qwen3-4B}), evaluating LSLC empirically against FP16 KV, INT4 KV, StreamingLLM, H2O, and 1D flat latent baselines across $1\text{K}$--$4\text{K}$ quality benchmarks and $32\text{K}$ live CUDA systems profiling.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{KV-Cache Eviction and Hierarchical Inference.}
StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2024streamingllm} stabilizes infinite generation by retaining attention sinks plus a sliding local window, while H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o} preserves heavy-hitter tokens accumulating high attention scores. Both approaches suffer irreversible information loss under aggressive eviction. \textbf{SeKV}~\cite{abaskohi2026sekv} eliminates permanent eviction via entropy-segmented GPU summary vectors and host-offloaded low-rank SVD bases reconstructed on demand. Concurrent late-2026 systems extend coarse-to-fine KV management across specialized architectures: \textbf{GroupKV}~\cite{wang2026groupkv} introduces group-level summary indexing and staleness repair for diffusion LLMs, while \textbf{LeanSparse}~\cite{pan2026leansparse} pairs layer-wise prefill streaming with coarse-to-fine decode indexing and host RAM offloading for Native Sparse Attention models. Unlike host-offloading or diffusion-specific managers, LSLC keeps the entire compressed book resident in GPU VRAM ($17.8$--$34.4\,\text{MiB}$ at $32\text{K}$ tokens across $1.7\text{B}$--$4\text{B}$ backbones) for standard autoregressive GQA Transformers. Along the low-rank KV projection axis, \textbf{SALS}~\cite{mu2025sals} observes that RoPE inflates key-vector rank and therefore performs RoPE-free sparse token selection inside a low-rank latent space before reconstructing selected token KV states ($6.4\times$ compression). Whereas SALS targets read-only low-rank reconstruction of standard token KV caches, LSLC compiles raw pages into a $132\times$--$830\times$ smaller 3D multi-resolution book supporting $O(1)$ in-place state revisions.

\paragraph{Latent Context Compression and Optical Rendering.}
AutoCompressors~\cite{chevalier2023autocompressors} and ICAE~\cite{ge2024icae} compress spans into 1D continuous memory slots consumed as soft prompts by a frozen decoder. \textbf{LCLMs}~\cite{li2026lclm} scale encoder-decoder latent compression to $>350\text{B}$ pretraining tokens, enabling agents to skim and expand compressed reasoning. Most recently, \textbf{Memorilla}~\cite{fallahpour2026memorilla} decouples persistent document memory from the frozen LLM context window by encoding text chunks once into a persistent latent store and projecting query-conditioned soft memory tokens at inference time. Along the orthogonal multi-agent communication axis, \textbf{XKV}~\cite{liu2026xkv} translates KV caches across heterogeneous frozen LLMs via dual-cache latent pooling and cross-layer mixing to avoid text-decoding overhead between collaborating agents. However, as our Track~0E and iso-budget 1D-vs-3D ablations confirm, flat 1D latent soft-tokens struggle on exact verbatim identifier extraction and lack 2D multi-resolution spatial pooling or structural layer disentanglement for prefix invariance under edits. Orthogonal visual approaches---DeepSeek-OCR~\cite{deepseek2025ocr} and Glyph~\cite{glyph2025}---render text into images for 2D VLM compression, incurring glyph overhead and degraded verbatim OCR accuracy at $20\times$ ratios. LSLC retains the 2D multi-resolution inductive bias via spatial pooling and 3D rotary geometry~\cite{su2024roformer} while avoiding RGB rendering entirely and pairing the 3D latent book with an $8\,\text{byte/token}$ \texttt{SparseExactBank}.

\paragraph{Mutable Neural Memory, Editable KV Caches, and Looped State Refinement.}
Test-time memory architectures such as \textbf{Titans}~\cite{behrouz2025titans} learn to update neural long-term memory weights online during inference, whereas Perceiver-style cross-attention architectures~\cite{jaegle2021perceiver} map arbitrary inputs onto fixed latent arrays. Closest to our editability motivation, Li~\cite{li2026notes} (\emph{Models Take Notes at Prefill}) shows that standard KV caches act as memoized prefill conclusions that can be amended via append-only CoT errata while maintaining high vLLM prefix-cache hit rates. Concurrently along the orthogonal axis of recurrent compute depth, \textbf{GLM-5.4}~\cite{zai2026glm54} (\emph{Loop Transformer}) demonstrates at $750\text{B}$-parameter scale that multi-pass hidden-state overwrites suffer severe activation-norm explosion unless governed by independent \emph{gated progressive updates} (conservative global context formation in Pass~1 followed by difficulty-sensitive local refinement in Pass~2), paired with a \emph{hybrid KV cache} that reuses Pass~1 sparse KV states and attention indices alongside a compact Pass~2 local window. LSLC independently uncovers and exploits this exact structural isomorphism along the \emph{context-memory axis}: executing \emph{in-place, $O(1)$-memory convex spatial delta overrides} ($\vect{Z}_{p,2} \leftarrow (1-\vect{G}_p)\odot\vect{Z}_{p,2} + \vect{G}_p\odot\Delta\vect{Z}_{p,2}$) inside a $132\times$--$143\times$ compressed 3D latent book while reusing a write-protected global prefix ($\text{TOC}_{1..P}$ and $\ell \in \{0,1\}$, $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0$) and sharing router \texttt{deep\_idx} indices across latent projection and $O(1)$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} gather.

Let an incoming token sequence $X = (x_1, \dots, x_N)$ be partitioned into modular pages of length $T_p = 256$ tokens, yielding $P = \lceil N / T_p \rceil$ pages with frozen token embeddings $\vect{E}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{T_p \times d_{\text{llm}}}$. LSLC encodes each page independently into a multi-layer 2D latent tensor $\vect{Z}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times C \times H \times W}$ ($L=3, C=128, H=4, W=4$) plus a sparse exact index $\mathcal{I}_p$, and decodes context dynamically on demand for a frozen LLM backbone (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).

\subsection{Pillar 1: Spatial-Layer Encoder \& 3D Factorized RoPE}
Inside \texttt{LayeredLatentEncoder}, the $T_p=256$ tokens of page $p$ are divided into $H \times W = 16$ ordered $16$-token discourse sub-spans so spatial neighborhoods $(h, w)$ preserve local sequential continuity under 2D pooling. A lightweight Perceiver-style cross-attention stack~\cite{jaegle2021perceiver} maps learnable queries $\vect{Q}_{\text{book}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(L \cdot H \cdot W) \times d_{\text{enc}}}$ over $\vect{E}_p$, followed by bottleneck projection to $C=128$ channels.

To encode layer identity alongside 2D spatial layout without confounding dimensions, internal encoder and delta-updater self-attention applies \textbf{3D Factorized Spatial-Layer RoPE}~\cite{su2024roformer}. Partitioning the rotary channels $d_{\text{rot}}$ into $(d_\ell, d_h, d_w)$, each latent slot at coordinate $(\ell, h, w)$ rotates its query/key sub-vectors by:
\begin{equation}
\vect{R}(\ell, h, w) = \mathrm{diag}\big(\vect{R}_{d_\ell}(\ell),\; \vect{R}_{d_h}(h),\; \vect{R}_{d_w}(w)\big),
\end{equation}
while cross-page routing uses a separate 1D page-index rotary offset $p \in \{0, \dots, P-1\}$ so pages remain modular and appendable without re-indexing prior pages.

\subsection{Pillar 2: Multi-Resolution Book Pyramid \& Matryoshka Zoom}
Given a page tensor $\vect{Z}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times C \times 4 \times 4}$, LSLC constructs three hierarchical reading resolutions using $O(L \cdot C \cdot H \cdot W)$ pooling:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Level 0 --- Table of Contents (TOC, $1\times 1$):} Global average pooling over the consolidated structural layers $\vect{Z}_{p, 0:2}$ ($\ell \in \{0,1\}$, $2C=256$ channels) produces $\vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}} = \mathrm{Proj}_{\text{TOC}}(\mathrm{Pool}_{1\times 1}(\vect{Z}_{p, 0:2})) \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_{\text{llm}}}$, emitting \textbf{$1$ token/page} permanently resident in LLM context while staying invariant under $\ell=2$ delta-updates.
    \item \textbf{Level 1 --- Skimming ($2\times 2$):} Stride-$2$ average pooling over $\vect{Z}_{p, 0:2}$ produces $\vect{U}_p^{\text{Skim}} \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times d_{\text{llm}}}$ (\textbf{$4$ tokens/page}) for moderately relevant pages.
    \item \textbf{Level 2 --- Deep Read (Layer-Ordered):} Preserves spatial structure with asymmetric per-layer resolution: $2\times 2$ ($4$ tokens) for Static ($\ell=0$), $2\times 2$ ($4$ tokens) for Entity ($\ell=1$), and full $4\times 4$ ($16$ tokens) for Dynamic working memory ($\ell=2$), totaling \textbf{$24$ dense tokens/page}.
\end{itemize}

At inference time, \texttt{QueryToTOCRouter} scores a user query $X_q = (q_1, \dots, q_M)$ (with mean embedding $\vect{q}$) against page $p \in \{1, \dots, P\}$ by combining dense \texttt{PageRoPE1D}-rotated TOC similarity with parameter-free in-VRAM late-interaction \texttt{MaxSim} over the page's $K_{\text{ex}}=16$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} token IDs $\mathcal{I}_p$ (resolved in $O(1)$ via the frozen LLM embedding table $\vect{e}(\cdot)$ at zero extra storage or CPU host-RAM cost):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_p &= \frac{(\vect{W}_q \vect{q})^\top (\vect{W}_k \vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}})}{\sqrt{d_r}} \\
&\quad + \alpha_{\text{sal}}\,\mathrm{zscore}_p\!\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}^*|}\sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}^*} \max_{\vect{v} \in \mathcal{V}_p} \cos\big(\vect{e}(q_m), \vect{v}\big)\bigg),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{V}_p = \{\vect{e}(\mathcal{I}_{p,j})\}_{j=1}^{K_{\text{ex}}} \cup \{\vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}}\}$ and $\mathcal{M}^* \subset \{1, \dots, M\}$ selects the top-$4$ highest-affinity query tokens, routing top-$k_{\text{deep}}$ pages ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{deep}}$) to Deep Read, next-$k_{\text{skim}}$ pages ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{skim}}$) to Skim, and remaining pages to TOC. (Optionally, an external lexical retriever such as Okapi BM25 can also override routing indices via \texttt{router\_out\_override}.) For a $16$-page ($4{,}096$-token) book ($k_{\text{deep}}=2, k_{\text{skim}}=2, 12$ TOC pages) plus $32$ exact-index tokens, active LLM tokens drop to $12 + 8 + 48 + 32 = 100$. For a $128$-page ($32{,}768$-token) book ($k_{\text{deep}}=2, k_{\text{skim}}=4, 122$ flat TOC pages), active tokens total $122 + 16 + 48 + 32 = 218$.

\paragraph{2-Level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page Hierarchical TOC.}
To prevent active TOC KV tokens from growing linearly as $O(P)$ and crowding self-attention above $32\text{K}$ tokens, LSLC partitions the $P$ pages into $C_{\text{chap}} = \lceil P / c \rceil$ contiguous chapters $\mathcal{P}_m = \{mc, \dots, \min(P, (m+1)c)-1\}$ of size $c$ ($c=16$ pages $= 4{,}096$ tokens). Each chapter $m \in \{0, \dots, C_{\text{chap}}-1\}$ forms a 1-token Chapter-TOC via mean pooling over its constituent page TOC vectors, and expands individual Page-TOCs exclusively within the active chapters $\mathcal{C}_{\text{sel}} = \{\lfloor p / c \rfloor \mid p \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{deep}}\}$ containing routed Deep Read pages:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vect{u}_m^{\text{Chap}} &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_m|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_m} \vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_{\text{llm}}}, \\
\text{TOC}_{\text{2-lvl}} &= \Big[\vect{u}_0^{\text{Chap}}, \dots, \vect{u}_{C_{\text{chap}}-1}^{\text{Chap}} \;\Big| \\
&\quad\;\; \big\{\vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}} : p \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{sel}} \setminus (\mathcal{D}_{\text{deep}} \cup \mathcal{D}_{\text{skim}})\big\}\Big],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}_{\text{sel}} = \bigcup_{m \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{sel}}} \mathcal{P}_m$. This bounds active TOC tokens by $\lceil P/c \rceil + k_{\text{deep}}(c-1)$ (shrinking the $32\text{K}$ active prefix from $218$ to $133$ tokens, $128\text{K}$ from $602$ to $157$, and $256\text{K}$ from $1{,}114$ to $189$) while strictly preserving $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0$ because every $\vect{u}_p^{\text{TOC}}$ pools exclusively over write-protected layers $\ell \in \{0,1\}$.

To prevent blurred zoom-out representations when pooling $4\times 4 \rightarrow 2\times 2 \rightarrow 1\times 1$, training enforces \textbf{Matryoshka Multi-Scale Supervision}: stochastic resolution dropout samples TOC-only, Skim, or Deep Read projections during optimization so a single page tensor remains decodable across every zoom level.

\subsection{Pillar 3: Anti-Collapse Layers, Prefix Caching \& Delta-Updates}
Unconstrained end-to-end training causes \emph{layer collapse}, mixing static and transient features indiscriminately across $\ell \in \{0,1,2\}$. LSLC enforces structural specialization via two inductive biases:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layer-Ordered Two-Bank Causal Prefix Alignment (Mode~A):} Soft-prompt tokens enter the frozen LLM \texttt{inputs\_embeds} in strict monotonicity of volatility, forming a \textbf{Two-Bank Prefix} over a single causal attention softmax:
    \begin{align}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{LLM}} = \Big[&\underbrace{\text{TOC}_{1..P} \mid \text{Skim} \mid \text{Deep}_{\ell=0} \mid \text{Deep}_{\ell=1}}_{\text{Bank 1: Invariant Structural Prefix}} \nonumber \\
    &\mid \underbrace{\text{Deep}_{\ell=2} \mid \mathcal{I}_{\text{deep}}}_{\text{Bank 2: Mutable}} \mid X_{\text{turn}}\Big].
    \end{align}
    Whenever a turn revision modifies only $\ell=2$, the LLM's KV prefix cache for the global $\text{TOC}_{1..P}$ block remains $100\%$ invariant across all queries and edits by structural construction ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0$, since TOC pools exclusively over $\ell \in \{0,1\}$). Furthermore, whenever consecutive turns inspect the same routed page set (\texttt{deep\_idx}, \texttt{skim\_idx}), the entire Bank~1 prefix ($\text{TOC}_{1..P} \mid \text{Skim} \mid \text{Deep}_{\ell \in \{0,1\}}$) remains bit-identical ($\max|\Delta_{\text{Bank1}}| = 0.0$), updating only Bank~2 at the tail of the prefix (whereas if a dynamic edit shifts runner-up \texttt{deep\_idx} selection across pages, only the newly routed runner-up $\text{Skim}/\text{Deep}_{\ell \in \{0,1\}}$ slice refreshes behind the immutable TOC prefix). Notably, this structure is the spatial-memory dual of the \emph{Two-Bank Sparse MLA} (\texttt{[Pass 1 Sparse Prefix | Pass 2 128-token Local SWA Window]}) independently introduced for recurrent layer looping in GLM-5.4~\cite{zai2026glm54}. (Mode~B replaces soft-prompt prefixes with gated cross-attention adapters~\cite{jaegle2021perceiver} in future work to eliminate causal ordering dependencies entirely.)
    \item \textbf{In-Place Sticky-Note Delta-Updates (Spatial Damp Gate with Pre-Gate RMS Alignment):} Given a revision turn $X_{\Delta}$ targeting page $p$ (selected by router top-$1$ or explicit page address), \texttt{DeltaUpdater} predicts a sparse update gate $\vect{G}_p \in [0, 1]^{H \times W}$ and raw override candidate $\widetilde{\Delta\vect{Z}}_{p,2} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$, aligns the candidate's channel-wise RMS norm to $\vect{Z}_{p,2}^{\text{old}}$ via $\Delta\vect{Z}_{p,2} = \widetilde{\Delta\vect{Z}}_{p,2} \odot \mathrm{clip}\!\big(\mathrm{RMS}(\vect{Z}_{p,2}^{\text{old}})/\mathrm{RMS}(\widetilde{\Delta\vect{Z}}_{p,2}),\, \frac{1}{4},\, 4\big)$, and applies the convex override exclusively to dynamic layer $\ell=2$:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:delta}
    \begin{aligned}
    \vect{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{new}} &= (1 - \vect{G}_p) \odot \vect{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{old}} + \vect{G}_p \odot \Delta\vect{Z}_{p, 2} \\
    &\equiv \vect{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{old}} + \vect{G}_p \odot \big(\Delta\vect{Z}_{p, 2} - \vect{Z}_{p, 2}^{\text{old}}\big), \\
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}} &= \lambda_{\text{sp}} \Phi_{[\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]}(\|\vect{G}_p\|_1),
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    where $\Phi_{[\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]}$ penalizes gate activation outside target sparsity band $[0.12, 0.26]$, while structural layers $\vect{Z}_{p, 0:2}$ ($\ell \in \{0,1\}$) remain strictly write-protected after initial consolidation. Algebraically, Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta} is identical to the recurrent \emph{Damp Gate} ($\vect{x}_t = \vect{x}_{t-1} + \beta_t(\vect{h}_t - \vect{x}_{t-1})$) used in GLM-5.4~\cite{zai2026glm54} to prevent activation-norm explosion across multi-pass hidden-state overwrites.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pillar 4: Sparse Exact-Recall Footnote Bank \& Objective}
Although a $12\,\text{KiB}$ FP16 page latent ($6{,}144$ continuous scalars) occupies more raw bytes than $256$ discrete \texttt{int32} token IDs ($1\,\text{KiB}$), continuous soft-prompt vectors must project into distributed $d_{\text{llm}}$-dimensional hidden semantics rather than indexing the discrete vocabulary table directly. Consequently, pure continuous bottlenecks suffer an optimization and representation-alignment bottleneck on arbitrary high-entropy identifiers (hex hashes, random numeric codes, snake\_case symbols). To decouple semantic compression from verbatim token copy without falling back to full-page raw-text re-prefill (which would consume $256$ active KV tokens per retrieved page rather than $24 + 16 = 40$), \texttt{SalienceGate} scores every raw token $i$ in page $p$ via:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_i &= \sigma\big(\vect{w}_s^\top \vect{e}_i + \gamma H_{\text{surprisal}}(x_i) \\
&\quad + \beta_{\text{code}}\mathbb{I}_{\text{code}}(i) + \beta_{\text{hdr}}\mathbb{I}_{\text{hdr}}(i)\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
combining learned projection, rare-token surprisal $H_{\text{surprisal}}(x_i)$, dense numeric/identifier span detection $\mathbb{I}_{\text{code}}(i)$, and a structural right-of-header window $\mathbb{I}_{\text{hdr}}(i)$ ($12$ tokens following \texttt{`:'} or \texttt{`]'
} delimiters to capture static $\ell=0$ policy values alongside exact codes), selecting the top-$\rho$ ($\rho = 6.25\%$, i.e., $16$ tokens per $256$-token page) into compact $8$-byte records $(\texttt{token\_id}\!:\!\texttt{int32},\allowbreak\;\texttt{page\_id}\!:\!\texttt{uint16},\allowbreak\;\texttt{h}\!:\!\texttt{uint8},\allowbreak\;\texttt{w}\!:\!\texttt{uint8})$ consuming only $128\,\text{bytes/page}$ ($16\,\text{KiB}$ for $32{,}768$ tokens). When page $p$ enters Deep Read, embeddings are gathered in $O(1)$ directly from the frozen LLM's \texttt{embed\_tokens} table, summed with bounded $(h, w)$ spatial tags, and weighted by relative router confidence $(p_{\text{deep}}/p_{\max})^2$ prior to $X_{\text{turn}}$.

All trainable LSLC modules ($40.42\text{M}$ trainable parameters on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and $53.52\text{M}$ on \texttt{Qwen3-4B}; base LLM strictly frozen) are optimized jointly via:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{NTP}} + \lambda_{\text{rec}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{autoenc}} + \lambda_{\text{mat}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Matryoshka}} \\
&\quad + \lambda_{\text{sp}}\|\vect{G}\|_1 + \lambda_{\text{route}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{router}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
combining next-token prediction, page autoencoding reconstruction, multi-scale zoom distillation, delta-gate $L_1$ sparsity, and auxiliary router supervision.

\subsection{Key Empirical Findings}
\paragraph{1.\ 3D Spatial-Layer Structure vs.\ 1D Flat Latents (Track~0E, Track~2 \& Iso-Budget Ablation).}
Holding the latent byte budget strictly equal at $12\,\text{KiB/page}$ ($24$ projected tokens/page), replacing LSLC's 3D $[\ell, h, w]$ grid and \texttt{FactorizedRoPE3D} with an unindexed 1D flat latent compressor (Track~0E, analogous to ICAE/Memorilla chunk slots) degrades WikiText-2 perplexity from $\mathbf{15.66}$ to $24.05$ ($+8.39$ PPL penalty), drops Semantic NIAH from $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ to $46.7\%$, and completely breaks prefix invariance when a page is edited ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 1.097$ vs.\ $\mathbf{0.0}$ in LSLC). Furthermore, our strictly controlled iso-parameter ($40.42\text{M}$), iso-byte ($12.125\,\text{KiB/page}$), iso-step, and iso-\texttt{SparseExactBank} ablation in Table~\ref{tab:unified_eval} (Panel~C) isolates each structural component: equipping \texttt{1D-Flat} ($[1, 128, 48]$) with \texttt{SparseExactBank} lifts its $1\text{K}$ Exact NIAH Substring EM to $73.3\%$, yet \texttt{1D-Flat} still fails TOC prefix invariance ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.754$), achieves only $66.7\%$ Semantic NIAH, and suffers $22.41$ PPL; adding 3-layer separation without 2D spatial pooling (\texttt{1D-3Layer}, $[3, 128, 16]$) restores $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$ and lifts Semantic NIAH to $93.3\%$ ($24.86$ PPL, $62.5\%$ static $\ell=0$ retention); and full \texttt{3D-Spatial} ($[3, 128, 4, 4]$ + \texttt{FactorizedRoPE3D}) reduces WikiText-2 perplexity to $\mathbf{18.94}$ ($-3.47$ to $-5.92$ PPL points), reaches $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ Semantic NIAH, and restores $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ static $\ell=0$ retention with $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=\mathbf{0.0}$.

\paragraph{2.\ Information-Theoretic Rescue via 8-Byte \texttt{SparseExactBank} (Track~1 vs.\ Track~2).}
On continuous 3D latents alone (Track~1), both backbones achieve $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ Semantic NIAH EM (surpassing StreamingLLM at $53.3\%$/$6.7\%$ and H2O-$20\%$ at $53.3\%$/$0.0\%$), confirming zero semantic context loss across all needle depths. However, continuous $C=128$ bottlenecks achieve $0.0\%$ Exact NIAH string match ($24.7\%$--$27.3\%$ token accuracy) on 9-token random numeric codes. Activating the $8\,\text{B/token}$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} ($128\,\text{B/page}$, Track~2) achieves $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ needle token capture rate, boosting teacher-forced Exact NIAH token accuracy to $\mathbf{90.7\%}$ on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} (matching Full KV's $90.0\%$) and lifting autoregressive Exact String Match by $\mathbf{+66.7}$ percentage points ($0.0\% \rightarrow \mathbf{66.7\%}$) on both \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and \texttt{Qwen3-4B}.

\paragraph{3.\ Positive Scaling Law \& Sparse-Footnote Fluency Trade-Off (\texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{Qwen3-4B}).}
Scaling the frozen backbone from $1.7\text{B}$ to $4\text{B}$ improves LSLC Track~1 perplexity from $16.59 \rightarrow \mathbf{12.88}$ (outperforming both StreamingLLM at $13.64$ and H2O-$20\%$ at $14.27$) and Track~2 perplexity from $15.66 \rightarrow \mathbf{14.24}$ (with pure \texttt{deep\_only} zoom reaching $\mathbf{13.51}$ and \texttt{toc\_only} reaching $15.78$). Notably, whereas adding \texttt{SparseExactBank} on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} (300 steps, $40.42\text{M}$ params) reduces mixed-mode PPL ($16.59 \rightarrow 15.66$), on the wider \texttt{Qwen3-4B} projector ($d_{\text{llm}}=2560$, $53.52\text{M}$ params trained under a fixed 250-step budget) injecting discrete verbatim footnote tokens introduces a modest general-prose fluency trade-off in mixed shallow/deep mode ($12.88 \rightarrow 14.24$ PPL, while \texttt{deep\_only} remains at $13.51$) alongside a slightly lower teacher-forced Exact NIAH token accuracy ($79.3\%$ vs.\ $90.7\%$ on $1.7\text{B}$), even as autoregressive Exact String Match ($66.7\%$) and needle capture ($100.0\%$) match $1.7\text{B}$. At $N=32{,}768$ tokens on \texttt{Qwen3-4B}, Full BF16 KV-cache requires $4{,}610.25\,\text{MiB}$ and $12{,}725.77\,\text{ms}$ ($12.73\,\text{s}$) TTFT, whereas LSLC consumes only $\mathbf{34.42\,\text{MiB}}$ ($\mathbf{133.93\times}$ smaller) with $\mathbf{111.83\,\text{ms}}$ Cold TTFT ($\mathbf{113.8\times}$ faster) and $\mathbf{84.30\,\text{ms}}$ Warm TTFT ($\mathbf{151.0\times}$ faster).

\section{Discussion, Error Decomposition \& Limitations}

\paragraph{3-Stage Error Decomposition on Multi-Page 4K Books (Track~3).}
On 16-page ($4{,}096$-token) books (Track~3 on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}), LSLC maintains $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ \texttt{SalienceGate} needle capture rate and $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ Semantic NIAH EM, while autoregressive Exact NIAH EM settles at $50.0\%$ ($70.0\%$ token accuracy). Telemetry decomposes this gap into three transparent stages: (1)~\emph{Intra-Page Capture} remains lossless at $100.0\%$ because \texttt{SalienceGate} operates page-modularly ($T_p=256$); (2)~\emph{Top-$k_{\text{deep}}$ Query-to-TOC Routing} ($k_{\text{deep}}=2$ of $16$ pages $= 12.5\%$ budget) achieves $40.0\%$ top-2 recall after 300 steps of light training, bounding exact footnote gather whenever the target page falls outside \texttt{deep\_idx}; and (3)~\emph{Cross-Page Attention Dilution} across $100$ active virtual tokens (including $16$ exact tokens from the runner-up \texttt{deep\_idx} page containing background Wikipedia dates/numbers).

\paragraph{Single-Page Confidence Weighting vs.\ Multi-Hop Trade-Off \& Static $\ell=0$ Replay (Track~4).}
In Track~4 ($N_{\text{train}}=96$, $N_{\text{eval}}=24$ held-out episodes, $k=3$ sequential in-place overrides per episode), \texttt{DeltaUpdater} maintains active spatial gate sparsity inside the target band ($\text{mean}(\vect{G}_p) = 0.191$--$0.259 \in [0.05, 0.35]$), achieves $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ held-out Delta-Edit Exact Match on both \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and \texttt{Qwen3-4B}, and preserves strict bit-identical TOC prefix invariance ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = \mathbf{0.0}$). Two architectural trade-offs surfaced during Track~4 analysis. First, while pre-override latent probes on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} retain static $\ell=0$ rules ($100.0\%$ EM), optimizing \texttt{DeltaUpdater} exclusively on override turns without joint static-query replay shifts un-replayed static readout ($0.0\%$ EM in single-task Track~4) even though $\vect{Z}_{p,0:2}$ remains bit-identical ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$). Adding the right-of-header \texttt{SalienceGate} window, joint static $\ell=0$ replay ($33\%$ episode mix), and freezing \texttt{projector.toc\_proj} during multi-scale router tuning completely resolves this drift in our unified \texttt{v2.0.0} checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:unified_eval}, Panel~B), restoring static $\ell=0$ retention to $\mathbf{97.2\%}$ Strict/Substring EM on $k=3$ overrides, $\mathbf{86.7\%}$--$\mathbf{90.0\%}$ on $k=5$ symbolic overrides, and $\mathbf{93.3\%}$--$\mathbf{96.7\%}$ on paraphrased natural-language overrides. Second, weighting footnotes by relative router confidence $(p_{\text{deep}}/p_{\max})^2$ suppresses distractor noise for single-page queries at the cost of attenuating secondary pages during equal-weight multi-hop synthesis, motivating independent sigmoid threshold gating in future extensions.

\paragraph{Adversarial Stress-Testing, $k=5$ Symbolic Overrides \& Constant-$k$ RAG Memory Crossover (Table~\ref{tab:hard_stress}).}
To test whether LSLC's editability and hybrid exact-recall mechanism survive beyond 4-page numeric templates, Table~\ref{tab:hard_stress} evaluates five stress regimes across \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} (\texttt{BF16}) and \texttt{Qwen3-8B} (loaded with 4-bit \texttt{NF4} weights and \texttt{BF16} runtime KV-cache on a single $22\,\text{GiB}$ L4 GPU). Pairing integer \texttt{PageRoPE1D} with quadratic runner-up confidence attenuation $(p_{\text{deep}}/p_{\max})^2$ suppresses distractor footnotes from secondary pages, reaching $\mathbf{90.0\%}$ episode EM ($\mathbf{96.0\%}$ token accuracy) on 8-page multi-file Python codebases (\textbf{EC-1}) on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ EM on \texttt{Qwen3-8B}, alongside $\mathbf{85.0\%}$ ($1.7\text{B}$) and $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ ($8\text{B}$) EM under four near-miss decoy keys (\textbf{EC-2}). On context-grounded multi-step reasoning (\textbf{EC-5}), \texttt{Qwen3-8B} reaches $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ accuracy matching Full KV. Under deep $k=5$ sequential symbolic overrides (\textbf{EC-3}), a cold-trained \texttt{DeltaUpdater} scores $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ EM ($93.75\%$ token accuracy) on \texttt{BF16} \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} and $55.0\%$ EM ($90.89\%$ token accuracy) on 4-bit \texttt{Qwen3-8B} due to cumulative pre-gate norm drift without an intermediate \texttt{RMSNorm}~\cite{zai2026glm54}.

Finally, live CUDA systems profiling from $32\text{K}$ to $256\text{K}$ tokens (Figure~\ref{fig:vram_crossover}) exposes both LSLC's Full-KV asymptote and its exact crossover against \textbf{Constant-$k$ Raw-Text Chunk RAG}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Against Full BF16 KV, a flat 1-level TOC emits $1$ active KV token per page alongside $12.125\,\text{KiB/page}$ storage, bounding 1-level compression as $P \to \infty$ by $\lim_{P\to\infty}\frac{256\cdot\text{KV}_{\text{tok}}}{12.125\,\text{KiB}+\text{KV}_{\text{tok}}} = \mathbf{231.0\times}$ ($1.7\text{B}$) and $\mathbf{236.1\times}$ ($4\text{B}/8\text{B}$).
    \item Standard \textbf{Constant-$k=2$ Raw-Text Chunk RAG} (zero TOC tokens in LLM KV; strictly $512$ retrieved raw tokens $= 57.89\text{--}58.88\,\text{MiB}$ live on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}, storing $1{,}152\,\text{B/page}$ raw IDs+index) keeps active KV constant, whereas flat 1-level LSLC grows by $127{,}104\,\text{B/page}$ ($1.7\text{B}$), crossing at:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    P^* &= \frac{(512 - 90)\cdot \text{KV}_{\text{tok}}}{12{,}416 + \text{KV}_{\text{tok}} - 1{,}152} \\
    &\Longrightarrow \mathbf{384.3\text{ pages} \;(\approx 98.4\text{K})} \text{ on } \texttt{1.7B}, \\
    &\hphantom{\Longrightarrow\;} \mathbf{392.1\text{ pages} \;(\approx 100.4\text{K})} \text{ on } \texttt{4B/8B}.
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    At $128\text{K}$ ($73.66\,\text{MiB}$) and $256\text{K}$ ($135.72\,\text{MiB}$), flat 1-level LSLC indeed exceeds Constant-$k=2$ RAG ($58.31$--$58.88\,\text{MiB}$ GPU VRAM plus $0.26$--$0.84\,\text{MiB}$ CPU BM25 host RAM). Activating our \textbf{2-level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page hierarchy} (\texttt{chapter\_size=16}) cuts live CUDA net VRAM on \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} to $\mathbf{16.17\text{--}17.81\,\text{MiB}}$ ($32\text{K}$, $118\text{--}133$ active prefix tokens depending on whether top-$2$ pages share a chapter, $3.25\times$--$3.58\times <$ RAG), $\mathbf{24.98\text{--}25.09\,\text{MiB}}$ ($128\text{K}$, $157\text{--}158$ tokens, $2.32\times$--$2.33\times <$ RAG), and $\mathbf{34.55\,\text{MiB}}$ ($256\text{K}$, $189$ tokens, $1.70\times <$ RAG; $829.9\times <$ Full BF16 KV), while maintaining $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = 0.0$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Unified Multi-Seed Evaluation, Pure GPU vs.\ Hybrid Routing, Equal-Token TOC Control \& 5-Way Editable RAG Frontier (Table~\ref{tab:unified_eval}).}
To provide a comprehensive, self-contained evaluation across retrieval and editing regimes, Table~\ref{tab:unified_eval} reports our unified \texttt{v2.0.0} checkpoint across three seeds ($\{42, 1337, 9999\}$, $N=180$ $32\text{K}$ NIAH, $N=60$ $128\text{K}$ NIAH, and $N=288$ state-override episodes on NVIDIA L4 \texttt{BF16}), reporting both normalized \textbf{Strict EM} and \textbf{Substring EM} (\texttt{target in prediction}) alongside leading-whitespace-stripped teacher-forced content-token accuracy:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{End-to-End Pure GPU Routing ($0\,\text{B}$ CPU RAM) vs.\ Hybrid Shared-BM25 ($270$--$857\,\text{KiB}$ CPU RAM):} By scoring query tokens directly in VRAM via late-interaction \texttt{MaxSim} over each page's $16$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} embeddings and $1$ TOC vector (Eq.~3), End-to-End 2-Level LSLC requires zero external lexical index and achieves $\mathbf{74.4\%}$ Strict / $\mathbf{97.8\% \pm 3.9\%}$ Substring Semantic EM at $32\text{K}$ ($\mathbf{46.7\%}$ Strict / $\mathbf{76.7\% \pm 15.3\%}$ Substring at $128\text{K}$) alongside $31.1\%$--$33.3\%$ Strict / $56.7\%$--$61.1\%$ Substring Exact EM ($70.0\%$--$73.3\%$ exact top-2 recall). Pairing LSLC with a shared CPU Okapi BM25 retriever ($97.2\%$--$100.0\%$ top-2 recall) further lifts Exact Substring EM to $\mathbf{82.2\% \pm 10.7\%}$ ($\mathbf{97.0\%}$ content-token accuracy) at $32\text{K}$ and $\mathbf{73.3\% \pm 11.5\%}$ ($\mathbf{94.1\%}$ content-token accuracy) at $128\text{K}$.
    \item \textbf{Decomposing 2-Level Hierarchical TOC via Equal-Token Truncated Flat Control ($133$/$158$ Tokens):} Comparing \texttt{LSLC (Shared BM25, Flat 1-Level TOC)} ($218$ tokens at $32\text{K}$; $602$ at $128\text{K}$) against an equal-token \texttt{Truncated Flat TOC Control} ($133$ tokens at $32\text{K}$; $158$ at $128\text{K}$) demonstrates that pruning unrouted TOC tokens is the primary mechanism eliminating self-attention dilution over Deep Read tokens (lifting $32\text{K}$ Semantic Substring EM from $40.0\% \rightarrow \mathbf{98.9\%}$ and $128\text{K}$ Semantic Substring EM from $20.0\% \rightarrow \mathbf{70.0\%}$). At equal token budget, 2-level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page pooling ($c=16$) contributes an additional $+1.2\%$ to $+3.3\%$ Strict EM gain ($44.4\% \rightarrow \mathbf{45.6\%}$ Exact and $72.2\% \rightarrow \mathbf{74.4\%}$ Semantic at $32\text{K}$; $40.0\% \rightarrow \mathbf{43.3\%}$ Exact at $128\text{K}$) while retaining coarse chapter summaries across $100\%$ of the book rather than dropping $70\%$--$86\%$ of pages from the global TOC.
    \item \textbf{5-Way Editable RAG Frontier---Why Deterministic Regex Patching Is Necessary to Test yet Insufficient for Paraphrased Edits:} In Panel~B, deterministic \textbf{Direct Text-Patch + BM25 Re-Index RAG} ($1.33$--$1.41\,\text{ms/edit}$) reaches $86.7\%$ Substring EM on structured $k=5$ symbolic templates where regex matches $100\%$ of edits (though only $0.0\%$--$3.3\%$ Strict EM because 512-token retrieved raw chunks induce verbose continuation decoding). However, when revisions arrive as \textbf{paraphrased natural-language prose} ($N=90$), deterministic regex matching drops to $0.0\%$ and Direct Text-Patch RAG collapses to $\mathbf{0.0\%}$ Strict and Substring EM. In contrast, LSLC \texttt{DeltaUpdater} ($\mathbf{8.62\text{--}8.70\,\text{ms/edit}}$ on L4, $57\times$--$1{,}294\times$ faster than 11-token block or 256-token full-page LLM rewrites) requires no schema rules, achieving $\mathbf{94.4\%}$ Strict / $\mathbf{95.8\%}$ Substring EM ($\mathbf{100.0\%}$ content-token accuracy) on $k=3$ numeric overrides, $\mathbf{38.3\%\text{--}43.3\%}$ Strict / $\mathbf{40.0\%\text{--}46.7\%}$ Substring EM ($\mathbf{82.0\%\text{--}86.6\%}$ content-token accuracy) on $k=5$ symbolic overrides, $\mathbf{35.0\%\text{--}40.0\%}$ Substring EM on paraphrased natural-language revisions (outperforming LLM-Rewrite RAG's $30.0\%$ Substring EM at $496.4\,\text{ms/edit}$), and $\mathbf{86.7\%\text{--}97.2\%}$ static $\ell=0$ policy retention EM with $\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}| = \mathbf{0.0}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

We presented the architecture, byte-level systems accounting, and empirical evaluation of \textbf{Layered Spatial Latent Caching (LSLC)} across \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B}, \texttt{Qwen3-4B}, and 4-bit \texttt{Qwen3-8B}. Combining a $[L=3, C=128, H=4, W=4]$ spatial-layer book with an $8\,\text{byte/token}$ \texttt{SparseExactBank} (supporting both $O(1)$ exact copy and $0\,\text{B}$-CPU in-VRAM \texttt{MaxSim} routing), volatility-ordered Two-Bank prefix caching ($\max|\Delta_{\text{TOC}}|=0.0$), RMS-aligned convex $\ell=2$ \texttt{DeltaUpdater} overrides, and a 2-level Chapter$\rightarrow$Page hierarchy above $\sim 100\text{K}$ tokens yields $1.70\times$--$3.58\times$ smaller active+store VRAM than Constant-$k=2$ Raw-Text RAG across $32\text{K}$--$256\text{K}$ ($221\times$--$830\times$ below Full BF16 KV), $97.8\%$--$98.9\%$ $32\text{K}$ Semantic Substring EM ($74.4\%$ Strict EM) and $82.2\%$ $32\text{K}$ / $73.3\%$ $128\text{K}$ Exact Substring EM, and $8.6\,\text{ms/edit}$ in-place state revisions ($57\times$--$1{,}738\times$ faster than LLM chunk rewrites) with $86.7\%$--$97.2\%$ static $\ell=0$ retention. Scaling learned semantic router recall at $\ge 128\text{K}$, periodic single-page reconsolidation for deep $k \ge 5$ edits, and Mode~B cross-attention adapters onto long-context suites such as RULER and LongBench marks the direct next step.
